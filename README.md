# Rock Base Brawl

A 1v1 first-person "gather, build, raid" game for Unity 6 (6000.0.42f1, URP) using **Netcode for GameObjects 2.4**.
Both players start with a rock, gather wood and stone, build a Rust-style base, craft tools and weapons, and fight over a ball that waits under a glass dome in the middle of the map.

## How to test

### Option A: two players inside the editor (Multiplayer Play Mode)
1. Open the project and open `Assets/Scenes/Game.unity`.
2. Open **Window > Multiplayer > Multiplayer Play Mode** and tick **Player 2**. Wait for it to boot.
3. Press Play. In the main editor window click **HOST GAME**. In the Player 2 window click **JOIN GAME** (IP `127.0.0.1`).

### Option B: standalone builds (best for LAN play with a friend)
1. **Rock Game > Build Windows Player**. The build goes to `Builds/Windows/Alien Rock Game.exe` (its saves still live under the old product name, Rock Base Brawl, so nobody's settings reset).
2. Run it twice (or on two PCs). One clicks **HOST GAME**, the other types the host's IP and clicks **JOIN GAME**.
   Port 7777/UDP must be reachable; allow the Windows firewall prompt.
   You can also press Play in the editor and host or join from there against a build.

### Option C: over the internet through Steam (no Hamachi, no port forwarding)
Both players open Steam and log in (any normal account), then run the same build. The host clicks **HOST**; in the
lobby, **INVITE FRIENDS** opens Steam's invite list (or the friend right-clicks the host on their friends list >
**Join Game**, or pastes the host's **COPY ROOM ID** - their SteamID - into JOIN). Traffic goes through Valve's free
relay. Solo, the tutorial and the `-autotest` runs stay on IP; with Steam closed (or `-nosteam`) everything works by IP
as before. Code: `SteamConfig.cs` (the app ID), `SteamBoot.cs`, `SteamTransport.cs`, `SteamLobby.cs`.
- It uses Valve's test app **480** ("Spacewar"), so Steam shows you as "playing Spacewar".
- **Moving to the game's own Steam app:** change `AppId` in `SteamConfig.cs` to the new number and rebuild. That's all.
  (The build writes `steam_appid.txt` next to the exe; leave that file out of what's uploaded to Steam.)

### Spectating
JOIN GAME on a match that has already started (or is full) no longer turns you away: you come in as a **spectator** (up to 8 per match). You get no body and no team and can't do anything in the world; the camera is a player's own first-person view (their eyes, where they're looking, what's in their hands). **Left click** watches the next player, **right click** the previous one. If the one you're watching dies you move on to someone alive after a moment; if they leave you move on at once. The HUD is just a bar along the bottom (who you're watching, the clock, LEAVE) and the chat. You see the victory cutscene and then the end screen ("BLUE WINS") with Leave game, like everyone else. Spectators never count as players (teams, the lobby countdown, who wins, someone leaving) and they're listed on their own SPECTATORS line under the scoreboard. A spectator stays a spectator for that match even if a place frees up. Code: `Spectator.cs`, `Hud.Spectator.cs`, `Bootstrap.Approve`. Test: `Tools\run_autotests.ps1 "spectate+client2:-fast"` (a 1v1 host, a client that plays, and a second client that joins the running match).

(`Stylize.shader`'s `_RgFarLine`: x the far thickness as a share of the near one, blended in by the pixel's distance; y how much a silhouette against the sky skips the distance fade.) **Energy wall** (`world.energywall`, off to start with): the glass wall between the halves and the ball's dome in the middle are drawn as **a glowing energy field** instead of glass - a faint cyan sheet with a honeycomb of light laid over it in world space (the panels and the dome line up), cells that flare up now and then, bright scan bands rising up it, fine flickering scanlines and a brighter rim where you see it edge-on; team-neutral, and the glass's frame lines and ribs are hidden. Only the look changes: it stops you and drops exactly like the glass (same colliders, same `GlassWallDrop`). Shader `Assets/Game/Resources/World/EnergyWall.shader` (in Resources, so it's in every build; loaded by `EnergyWall`), switched live both ways. `-autotest dome` checks it switches on and off with the colliders untouched and photographs it (`dome_*_energy_wall`).
  - **Voice chat** (proximity): Off / Open mic / Push to talk, the microphone, mic volume, open-mic sensitivity and a live mic level meter. You only hear people near you (3D, fades out by ~45 m). **Alien voice** (On / Off, saved like the other sound settings): your mic is pitched up 1.32x, ring-modulated at 48 Hz (45% deep, so the words stay clear) and run through a short flanger before it's sent, so everyone hears you as an alien. **Hear myself** (not saved) plays your own voice back exactly as the others get it (after the codec, the filter and a jitter buffer) - use headphones.
    - How it works (`VoiceChat.cs`, the signal path in `VoiceDsp.cs`): the mic is captured at 48 kHz when it can, low-passed (6th-order Butterworth) and resampled (Catmull-Rom) to **16 kHz**; each **20 ms frame** gets a 90 Hz rumble filter, the mic volume, a soft noise gate (-15 dB below the open-mic threshold, or a fixed floor on push to talk), **automatic gain** (pulls speech towards one loudness, 0.5x..3x: down fast, up over ~1.5 s), the alien filter if it's on and a **soft limiter** (nothing clips), then is packed as **IMA ADPCM** (4 bits a sample, 166-byte packets carrying their own codec state and a sequence number: 66 kbit/s while talking - the same bandwidth as the old 8 kHz mu-law, twice the audio bandwidth) and sent **unreliable** (a late voice packet is worse than a lost one). Open mic keeps 40 ms before the voice starts so the first word isn't cut. The server passes each packet on **only to players within 60 m** (`VoiceChat.RelayRange`), and a 3-byte marker when the talking stops. Each talker plays from their head through an **adaptive jitter buffer** (`VoiceJitterBuffer`): it waits just long enough to cover how unevenly packets arrive (+ one audio block + one frame; usually ~50-90 ms), puts packets back in order, covers a lost one with a faded copy of the one before, grows after a real underrun and shrinks again, and plays up to 4% faster when too much has piled up. Playback is a 3D AudioSource playing a steady 1.0 with a filter (`VoiceTap`) that multiplies in the voice on the audio thread - no streaming-clip read-ahead.
    - Before this: 8 kHz mu-law in 100 ms chunks, a box-filter downsample, a fixed 200 ms pre-buffer and a streaming AudioClip (which reads ahead on its own). `-autotest voice` measures both (see Project layout).

## Display settings code (COPY / PASTE SETTINGS)
Settings > Display > **COPY SETTINGS** gives a code like this (one `key = value` a line; `#` starts a comment, except the `#` a colour starts with):
```
ALIEN ROCK GAME display settings v1
# Settings > Display (everything except the screen settings), 2026-10-04 12:00
# One "key = value" per line. Paste it back with PASTE SETTINGS; unknown keys are ignored, missing ones are left as they are.
# ---- POST PROCESSING ----
post = on
post.bloom = on
post.bloom.strength = 0.65    # changed (default 0.5)
post.extra.outlines = on    # changed (default off)
post.extra.outlines.strength = 0.5
# ---- POST PROCESSING ON THE UI ----
ui.post = on    # changed (default off)
ui.post.world = on
ui.cel = on    # changed (default off)
ui.outline.width = 4    # changed (default 2)
ui.outline.colour = #000000
# ---- SHADOWS ----
shadows.darkness = 0.8    # changed (default 1)
...
# ---- WORLD COLOURS ----
colour.hands.team = on
colour.Sky = #8FC1E8
...
# 7 values changed from the defaults.
```
- **Values**: switches are `on` / `off` (also `true/false`, `yes/no`, `1/0`); numbers are plain (`.` decimal; a trailing `%` is divided by 100, a unit after the number like `120 m` or `4 px` is ignored) in the same units as the code shows (strengths 0-1, distances in metres, `ui.outline.width` the same numbers as the slider: x 1.333 is pixels at 1440p); colours `#RRGGBB`; `ui.font` and `ui.accent` by name (`Bahnschrift`, `Alien green`) or number. Values outside a setting's range are clamped; while Settings > Display is open the sliders snap a value to their steps.
- **Pasting** (`DisplayCode.Apply`): the header line (`ALIEN ROCK GAME display settings v<N>`) isn't required, but without it at least one known key is (so pasting random text is refused, nothing changed). **Unknown keys are ignored** (a code from a newer game still pastes what this one knows), **keys that aren't there keep their current values** (so a code can be just the three lines you want), a value that doesn't parse is skipped; the message counts them. Lines from the old world colours copy (`TreeTrunks = #4F8F2A`, `HandsUseTeamColour = false`) still work.
- **Making a code the game's defaults**: every number / switch default is in **one table**, `DisplayDefaults` at the top of `Assets/Scripts/DisplaySettings.cs` - one `const` per setting, each with its code key in a comment (`public const float BloomStrength = 0.5f; // post.bloom.strength`); the settings' own code (`UiLook.cs`, `PostFx.cs`, `WorldLook.cs`, `Hud.Fps.cs`, `Config.AlienOutline*`, the Defaults buttons) all read from it. The world colours' defaults are the `Add(...)` lines in `WorldColors.cs` (`colour.<Id>` = the slot whose id is `<Id>`): to move one, add `.StartAt("#RRGGBB")` to its line - **don't** change the `Add` colour itself, that's the slot's `Base` (the colour the models are built in; the tint is picked / Base), so changing it would change how every colour looks. A few settings keep their default next to their own code: `beams.*` (`BeamFx.cs`), `treex.*` (`TreeX.cs` - `TreeXGlowBase` is the 100% mark, not the default), `basefloor.*` (`BaseFloor.cs`). So: give Claude the code and say "make these the defaults" - only the `# changed` lines need moving. (The current defaults are the game's tuned look - a COPY SETTINGS export of 2026-10-06.)
- **Adding a new display setting to the code** (`DisplayCode` in `DisplaySettings.cs`): **one line** in `DisplayCode.Table()`, e.g.
  `Float("beams.strength", "BEAMS", DisplayDefaults.BeamStrength, () => GameSettings.BeamStrength, (v, save) => GameSettings.SetBeams(v, GameSettings.BeamFalloff, save));`
  (`Bool`, `Float`, `Colour`, or `Choice(key, group, names, default, get, set)` for a pick from a list; the group is the `# ---- ... ----` heading it's listed under; the setter gets `save` - pass it on so the tests can change things without saving), plus its default in `DisplayDefaults`. Or declare the setting as a self-saving **`DisplayPref`** field of `GameSettings` - `public static readonly DisplayPref.Float BeamStrength = new("beams.strength", "BEAMS", DisplayDefaults.BeamStrength, 0f, 1f);`, read `.Value`, change it with `.Set(v)` (saved in PlayerPrefs as `RockGame.Disp.<key>`; `DisplayPref.Changed` fires) - and it's in the code **with no registration at all** (`DisplayCode` finds every `DisplayPref` there is; the UI's own looks are done this way, see `UiLook.cs`). A new world colour slot (`ColorSlots.Add`) is in the code automatically too. Keep the key once chosen (it's what old codes use); the screen settings (window, resolution, refresh rate) stay out on purpose.

## Airdrops
Each airdrop gets **one warning, 15 seconds before its ship comes in** (`NetGame.DropWarning`; it used to count down to the crate landing): a banner **AIRDROP IN 15 SECONDS** saying where it's coming down (*"It's dropping in the centre of the map!"* with Where = Middle of the map; every side / somewhere on the map otherwise), plus a line in the chat. There's no airdrop timer on the HUD and no other countdown. Once the wall is down, a giant alien ship comes down from very high up (in over the nearest edge of the map, taking **8 s** - half the speed it used to, `AirdropShip.Arrive`), hovers **over the map's glass dome** (clear of the glass, its open doors too - `AirdropShip.HoverAt`; **70 m up** - it used to be 45 - but never above 88 m for the height alone, so its top stays well under the clouds and nowhere near the Highlands' sky planets; higher only where the dome's glass makes it), **opens a round hatch in its belly** (two half-round doors swing down, the hole glows purple), and **a round hole opens in the glass under it first** (`MapDome.SetHole`: it opens out with its edge glowing purple, and a **rim in the same style as the glass's frame lines** - a wide band and a thin line just outside it - runs round it; the frame lines that crossed it go too). **Only once the hole is all the way open does the purple beam come out** (`AirdropShip.BeamStart`; it used to come down onto the glass and cut the hole while it shone) - straight down through the hole to a random spot (never close to a base; follow the purple beam) and **beams an airdrop crate** out of the hatch, **down through the hole**. The beam is glowing light (like the ball's beacon, `BeamFx`): a wide purple column, a bright white-violet core and a faint halo round it, with bands of light running down it and a glow on the ground where it lands, so it's easy to spot from across the map - as bright as the ball's beacon from far away, fading down to a faint purple column as you come up to it (Settings > Display > Beams). The ship **shines light down out of its belly** all the time it's there (a real spotlight onto the ground under it, brighter with the hatch open, a soft purple glow under the hull and a ring of glowing lights round its underside), and **a soft light under it shines back up at the hull**, so its underside is lit and doesn't melt into the sky. The crate comes down the beam at **half the old speed** (8 s; the crate lands 17 s after the ship appears), and **the beam hums while it's lowering the crate** (`Sfx.BeamHum`, a looping 3D sound at the drop spot: louder the closer you get, heard from about 95 m): it's the real crate's **solid** look (not a see-through ghost), lit by a little white light on the side you're looking from, and **the beam goes see-through round it** (the shader's `_Clear` points), so you can watch it come down inside the light. Once it's landed its beacon is a beam too (`Container.CrateBeacon`) - **it stops under the belly of the ship until that has flown off** (`AirdropShip.BeaconCeiling`; it used to run straight up through the UFO), then shoots up to its full height. Once it's down and the beam is off, **the hole is patched** (it closes back up, its edge glowing, in about 1.5 s), the hatch shuts, and **only then** does the ship **fly off over the edge of the map, shrinking smoothly into the distance** until it's gone (no popping out). Its whole path stays low: it comes in from about 110 m over its hover point and leaves almost level, under the clouds (never above 98 m, `AirdropShip.LeaveCeiling`), well clear of anything far off in the sky. *Why it used to pop out:* (1) it climbed 60 m as it left - right up into the cloud layer (105-160 m, clouds 2-4x size) - so from far away, where you look at it nearly sideways through the clouds, a cloud swallowed it while it was still big; (2) each lane had one ship picture, torn down and rebuilt whenever the lane started its next drop, so a drop launched within ~9 s of the last one landing (several airdrops per match, fast timers, the dev button) deleted the leaving ship on the spot. Now every flight is its own object that finishes its exit even after its lane has moved on (each with its own hole in the dome). It's all driven by the drop's synced start time, so the host and every client see the same hole at the same moment. The dome's collider stays whole while the hole is open (the crate coming down is only a picture - the real one appears on the ground when the beam gets there), so nothing can get out through it. How many come per match and which items they can have are set in **MODE OPTIONS**, and how common each one is in **CHANGE VALUES > Airdrop rarity**; a crate nobody emptied stays put. ## Game settings (CHANGE VALUES)
**CHANGE VALUES** on the main menu lists every stat: timings, airdrops, player HP and speeds, damage and gather rate for every tool, node HP, building costs and HP, crafting costs and more. They're folded into sections: click a heading to open it (each shows how many values it has and how many you changed). **Search** (the box at the top, **x** clears it): type and the list keeps only the values whose name or section has every word you typed (any case, spaces optional - `slide`, `bow speed`, `airdrop`), with the sections that have a match open under their heading ("8 of 27") and the matching words in gold; it scrolls back to the top as you type and the mouse wheel scrolls the results. Only the values you change are saved on your PC (so new defaults still reach you). When you host, your values are used for the whole match and sent to everyone.

- **Airdrop rarity** is **not in CHANGE VALUES any more: it's on the MODE OPTIONS screen**, as each item's drop chance with - / + (`Hud.IsModeOptionsSection`). It's still a weight for every airdrop item (Rarity C4, Rarity Death Wand, ... all 10 to start with, still host values sent to everyone and in the exported file): an item with 20 comes twice as often as one with 10, 0 never comes. Only the items ticked in MODE OPTIONS are in the draw (if they're all 0 they're equally likely). Used by airdrop crates, the respawn-loot option and Fun Random (`Cfg.PickAirdropItem`, `Cfg.AirdropChance`).
- **Export to a file** writes `RockBaseBrawl-settings.txt` next to the game (the project folder in the editor): every value, plus the main menu choices and mode options, with the changed ones marked. Send it over and say what it's for - new default settings, or the base of a new game mode.
- **Import from the file** reads it back in; **Open the folder** shows where it is.

## Inventory
- Wood, stone, arrows and berries are real items that take up slots. Tools and weapons go to the hotbar first.
- Materials stack onto what you already have; otherwise they go on the hotbar from **slot 6 (the last) backwards** (never into the empty slot you're holding your rock in), then the main inventory.
- Everything that goes into your inventory pops up in the bottom right ("+30 Wood"); materials spent on building and crafting show up there in red ("-15 Wood").
- Chests and high external walls can go right up against your walls and the machine: if the exact spot is a little too tight they slide into the nearest free spot (up to 0.7 m).
- A chest you open shows up to the **right** of your inventory.
- You always hold something: the hotbar only scrolls through slots that have an item. The rock stays on your hotbar - it can't be dropped or put in a chest.
- **Drag an item outside the inventory** to throw it on the ground; look at it and press E to pick it up.
- **Six across**: the hotbar (6, keys 1-6), the bag (3 rows of 6) and chests (2 rows of 6), with bigger slots than the old seven-wide grid. (`Cfg.HotbarSize` / `MainSize` / `ChestSlots`: 6 / 18 / 12, so 24 player slots on the network.)
- Drag to move or swap, **right-drag** to split a stack in half, **shift-click** to quick-move (between chest/bag and inventory, or hotbar and inventory). **Shift+drag** quick-moves every slot the pointer passes over (each slot once per drag, so you can sweep your whole bag into a chest; fast moves don't skip slots, and something that just moved into a slot isn't carried back). Under the bag it always says just, in white: *Drag to move* / *Right+Drag to Split* / *Shift+Click/Drag to quick-move*. A dragged item is just its icon on the pointer (no slot background behind it).
- **Items fly to their slot**: whatever moves (a drag, a split, shift-click into or out of a chest, a chest sorting itself) flies from where it was - or from the pointer, when you dragged it - to its new slot in a quick arc (0.24 s) and pops as it lands (a quick 0.08 s pop) (`Hud.Flights.cs`: worked out on your screen by comparing the slots with the frame before, so the server's rules don't change).
- **Right-drag shows the half on the pointer** from the moment you pick it up (and the slot keeps the other half) while you choose where it goes.
- **Point at anything** (an item in the bag or chest, or a crafting row) and its **name** (white) and **description** (grey) show under the crafting list (under the chest when one is open).
- **Shift-clicking things into a chest sorts it**: stacks of the same thing are topped up and everything is laid out in order - wood and stone, ammo, weapons, tools, things you place, armour, food, then the rest. (Dragging things in by hand leaves them where you put them.)
- **Storage chest**: craft it (TAB), hold it and click to place it anywhere inside your base. Press E on it to open it next to your inventory (12 slots: 2 rows of 6). Enemies who break in can loot it, and breaking it spills its contents into a bag. C4 only breaks one it's stuck right onto (not through a wall); rockets, the ram and melee break it as before.

## Crafting (TAB) and the trade stations (the code's workbenches)
**Names:** in the game the workbenches are the **Trade Station** (Workbench T1, `Item.Workbench`) and the **Trade Station 2** (Workbench T2, `Item.Workbench2`) - every item name, HUD message, list heading (TRADE STATION / TRADE STATION 2) and prompt says so, and the unlock banner is **TRADE STATION UNLOCKED**. Their descriptions are "Lets you obtain more stuff by trading resources intergalactically." and "Lets you obtain EVEN MORE STUFF by trading resources intergalactically." The code keeps its identifiers (`Workbench`, `BenchTier`...), and "workbench" / "Workbench T1 / T2" below means these. **Screens:** the tablets on the top are tilted towards you now (you used to see their backs), the Trade Station has **one monitor** on its back edge showing an **animated alien face on a call** (it sways, blinks and talks; every 6-13 s the call drops in static and a different-coloured alien picks up), and the Trade Station 2 has **dual monitors**: the alien call and a **fake stock-market screen** (a scrolling graph that's green while it's up and red while it's down, volume bars, a ticker). The pictures are a 32 x 24 texture redrawn 8 times a second while on camera, emissive so they read at night (`BenchScreen.cs`); local decoration, nothing synced.

**TAB** shows your inventory with the crafting list on its right, one row per item: its icon, name, price (red when you're short) and a **CRAFT** button (green when you can make it). Point at a row (or an item in your bag) and its name and description show under the list - that's all that's there (no notes about workbenches or where things can be crafted). Everything goes straight into your inventory (armour goes straight on; Builder's Fortify just happens). The rows don't add notes like "goes straight on" / "comes empty". The base upgrades (Fortify All Walls, the Wood Gen) aren't in this list: they're in **UPGRADES**, E on your upgrade station (left of the alien machine). Spears and hatchets can be crafted anywhere, the rest inside your base (Builder: anywhere, with its timers).

What's in the list depends on your team's **workbenches** - nothing is made AT a bench, it just unlocks more of the list:
- **Always (BASICS)**: Hatchet, Spear, Building Plan, Bow, Arrows, Storage Chest, Battering Ram and the **Workbench T1** - which is **locked for everyone until the ball has been captured by any team**: a team puts it in its machine's socket once, **or** keeps it in its base for **10 seconds** in all (lying there, in the socket, or carried by one of that team - each team's seconds add up over the match, they don't have to be in one go; an enemy carrying it through doesn't count). **The first capture unlocks the Workbench T1 for every team at once**, not just the team that made it (it used to be per team). Until then its row is grey and says "Can only craft once the ball has been captured" (pointing at it shows the seconds so far), and the server refuses it. When it unlocks, everyone in the match gets **TRADE STATION UNLOCKED - craft it in your bag** (a banner and a chime). Per team, synced (`NetGame.BenchUnlocks`, `NetGame.Bench.cs`; the seconds are **Bench Unlock Seconds** in CHANGE VALUES > Crafting). It stays unlocked for the rest of the match. **Builder** has no bases or machines, so it's never locked there.
- **TRADE STATION** (Workbench T1; 150 wood, Workbench Wood): Sword*, Crossbow, Armour, High External Wall, Chainsaw, Saddle (Builder: Fortify All Walls*) and the **Workbench T2**.
- **TRADE STATION 2** (Workbench T2; **2000 wood**, Workbench2 Wood; needs the T1 down first): Shotgun Shell*, Revolver Bullet*, Waterpipe Shotgun*, Revolver*, Alien Helmet*, C4* (ammo, then guns, then the helmet, then C4), then the Stone Pickaxe and the boat (water maps).
- (* power items: Arsenal, Auto Wood and Builder only.)
- A tier's items show **once your team has that bench down**, wherever you are, but can only be crafted **while you're inside your own base** (Builder: anywhere once it's down). Out of your base they're still listed, greyed with "in your base" (and the server refuses them); with no bench you only see the basics. Once a bench is down it drops off the list (one of each per team).
- Each tier has its own heading (BASICS / TRADE STATION / TRADE STATION 2). It's **one column**: when it's longer than the screen it **scrolls** - the mouse wheel over it (also while you're sprinting: Shift turns the wheel sideways on Windows, so that counts too), or the **scroll bar** that shows up on its right (drag the thumb, or click the track to jump there); a thin line at the top / bottom edge shows there's more that way. The bag always opens at the top of the list. (Checked at 1280x720 and 1920x1080.)
- A row you can't craft says why: the price in red (short of it), "in your base", "maxed out", "wearing it", "queue full", **"bag full"** (no room for it - before, CRAFT stayed green and nothing happened), or the locked workbench.
- **Putting a bench down**: hold it and click - **anywhere inside your own base** (grass, foundations or the bedrock), not over the spawn spot or the ball socket (Builder: anywhere). ## Gathering
- Normal graphics: Plains and Highlands are a thick, stylised meadow - tall blades of grass, dark at the root and bright at the tip, that sway in the wind and part round players, horses, chests, the ball and dropped items; patches of **tall golden wheat** (fewer of them than there were, further apart; each one has a knee-high rim of longer, green-gold grass round it and then **a clear step up to the tall middle** - head high and more, about 1.3 to 3 m, tallest in the very middle - so you can see where the tall grass starts; you can hide in it standing or crouching; it only parts a little as you push through, and there's none near the bases or the ball zone. How: the patches are the tops of a slow noise; the grass field stores how far in from its patch's edge each spot is (a distance transform), and the shader steps the height up a third of the way in (at most 3 m); patches too small to have a tall middle are dropped), clearings of short grass with white daisies, purple (and some pink and white) lupins and clumps of broad leaves - over ground of one flat colour (Highlands' steep rock is one flat grey). No grass on the bases, the ball drop zone, steep rock, or under anything built: **every building piece, chest, workbench and gamble machine keeps the grass off its footprint** (turned with it, out to 0.9 m round it, so no blade pokes through the mesh; not under an upper floor) while it stands, and **the grass grows back when it's destroyed** (`GrassField.Block` / `Unblock`: the grass field's G is worked out again just under the piece that changed, from the field as it was generated, and the texture is uploaded once a frame at most). Fallen logs the same, and the grass round them lies flat.
- **Birds** (`Birds.cs`, every graphics mode): each bird is a little low-poly 3D model (a rounded body with a head and a fanned tail, an orange beak, wings folded down its flanks while it sits), nestled in the needles on a branch of the tree's two lowest tiers (`ResourceNode.BirdPerch`: right on a branch's ridge, never in the air between two branch tips). When the tree is hit they **burst out from under the leaves all round the tree at once**, then **head off for the next tree straight away, each on its own line, and merge into one flock as they go** (`BirdFlock.BurstTime` / `GatherTime`: one flock about 2.4 s after the burst; they used to group up beside the tree first and only then leave). **flocks of 5-9 little dark birds sit on the branches of a few trees** (4 flocks per team about the map; the server puts them in trees nobody is within 45 m of - `ResourceNode.ServerFlocksTick` - so **you can always see beforehand that a tree has birds in it**). **A tree with no birds in it never sends any up** (a hit used to have a 15% chance of conjuring a new flock out of any tree, so one tree could send up flock after flock). **The first hit on their tree** - a chop, or **an arrow, crossbow bolt, thrown spear or bullet hitting it** (`ResourceNode.ServerStruck`, called from `ArrowHitRpc`, `SpearLandRpc`, `FirePistolRpc` and `SniperFireRpc` with the tree they reported) - sends the flock flapping up out of it with a burst of wing flaps and chirps; they fly in an arc to **another tree a long way off** (45-120 m away - it used to be 15-70 - or the furthest there is) and settle on its branches (a chirp as they land). **Hit that tree and they fly on to another**, and so on (felled, or flattened by an airstrike, the same). Everyone sees the same flock: the server picks the trees and the flight time and sends one message (from, to, how many, a seed); each player flies the birds along the same paths, and the tree they land in keeps how many sit in it (`ResourceNode.Birds`), so someone joining later sees them too. Each bird is a tiny body and two flapping wings (no shadows).
  - **Inside the tall wheat (or any grass) the blades right at your eyes fade out**: blades closer than ~0.3 m aren't drawn and they fade in (a fine dither pattern, so they stay solid and sorted) out to ~1.7 m - measured with the height counted double, so the grass at your feet stays. **The tall grass in a patch fades much more**: none closer than ~0.8 m, and it only comes back fully at ~3.4 m (staying see-through for longer on the way: that fade is squared). Standing or crouching in the wheat, your screen isn't covered and you can see out round you. It's only round your own camera: everyone else still sees you hidden in it. (`_GrassNearFade`, set from `GrassField.NearFadeFrom / NearFadeTo` and `NearFadeTallFrom / NearFadeTallTo`; `Grass.shader` clips in every pass.)
  - **Trampled grass** (like Rust): wherever players, horses, cars and the ball go, and where dropped items and chests lie, the grass is pressed flat (each blade falls its own way, a little paler) and stands back up by itself: it stays flat for ~4 s after the last step and is fully up again ~15 s later. The tall wheat isn't flattened. How: a "trample map" (one value every 0.5 m over the whole map: when that spot was last stepped on) that the grass shader reads, so nothing has to update while it recovers; ten times a second whatever moved stamps the strip it went over, and only the small squares that changed are copied to the GPU (16x16 blocks, `Graphics.CopyTexture`). Each PC tracks its own (it's only looks).
  - Kept fast (it's tuned on an integrated Intel GPU: about half a millisecond a frame at the default settings): one 8 m patch of blades is drawn on every 8 m square in view near you, in one draw per level of detail (procedural instancing); further out there are fewer, wider blades, single triangles past ~20 m, and past ~85 m the same patch is stretched over 16 m and then (past ~240 m) 32 m squares, so the number of squares stays small even at 270 m; the grass shrinks smoothly into the ground (no popping) and is gone by the render distance picked in Settings > Display (60 m to start with, up to 270 m; the flowers stop at 90 m). The density setting draws only the first share of each patch's blades (they're stored in random order, so it thins evenly) and picks the cheaper patch meshes. Blades are lit per vertex. Where grass grows comes from a 1-texel-a-metre "grass field" texture (height, how much grass, how far into a wheat patch, short grass) that the shader reads, so the same patch follows the hills. Code in `GrassField.cs` and `Assets/Game/Resources/Grass/` (`Grass.shader`). Hidden in PSX and AI PSX.
- Normal graphics, the rest of the world (`WorldDressing.cs`, `WorldLook.cs`, shader `Assets/Game/Resources/World/Painted.shader`: vertex-coloured meshes - the pines and the clouds smooth-shaded (`MeshKit.SmoothNormals`: each corner's normal is the average of the faces meeting there, except across sharp edges) - lit like the grass, with the wind done in the vertex shader):
  - **Pine trees** are tall (10-16 m) and come in **five kinds**, picked from each tree's seed (so every player sees the same tree): *classic* (four even tiers), *spruce* (slim, six narrow bluish tiers), *fir* (full and bushy, five wide flared tiers starting low), *droopy* (bell-shaped tiers whose branch tips hang down) and *scraggly* (sparser, uneven olive tiers higher up an older trunk with a few bare, broken-off branches). Each has a touch of colour of its own. A tier is a star-shaped skirt of branch tips, a ring part way up and the tip, with a shallow cone underneath. Needles and trunk are one mesh (two draws: needles, bark - tinted by the Pine needles / Tree trunks colours), **smooth-shaded** (the light rolls round the trunk and over each tier; a tier's rim over its underside stays a crisp edge), and **sway together** in the same gusts as the grass (taller trees a little more), shaking together when hit.
    - **Every hit on a tree shakes a few leaves loose**: 4-7 little flakes in that tree's own needle colour flutter down from just under its lowest tiers (mostly on your side), swinging side to side and tipping as they drift down, and lie on the ground for a moment before they shrink away (`FxLeaf` in `Fx.cs`; played with the hit's wood chips, so everyone who sees the hit sees them; PSX trees and palms drop them from low in their crown).
    - **The trunk never shows through the needles** (`ResourceNode.PineShape`): inside the crown it's never thicker than the tiers round it can hide (less a margin for the branch tips' flutter), so it tapers up through the crown and stops a little way into the top tier; it's part of the swaying mesh, so the needles can't slide off it in a gust. *Why it used to show:* the trunk was Unity's built-in cylinder, which is 2 units wide (not 1), so it was really 1.2 m thick - twice what the tier layout allowed for - and it stuck out between every pair of tiers (and it stood still while the needles swayed). The trunk is still 1.2 m thick (a flared, ten-sided log) where you hit it, and the collider reaches out to the bark's ten corners and 2 cm past (0.65 m radius, as tall as the trunk inside the needles: the X sits on the corners, so aimed at side on it still has to hit the tree - at 0.6 m the swing grazed past an X on the side of the trunk); the weak spots are where they were. **The X sits on the bark you see, never in it**: it's placed against the trunk mesh's own triangles, facing straight out, as far out as the bark comes anywhere behind it (`ResourceNode.BarkSpot`), worked out in the tree's own space so it shakes with it exactly. *Why it was sometimes inside:* it was put on the round collider, but the trunk has ten flat sides, so wherever a spot faced one of its corners the bark stood out past the collider and the middle of the X was buried in it (and while the tree shook, the collider lagged a frame behind).
    - `-autotest world` checks it for real: every tree's needles and bark are moved on the CPU exactly as the shader moves them (`WorldLook.PaintedWind`) at 16 moments of the wind, and every bit of bark above the lowest underside has to be inside a tier; then one tree of each kind (and the shortest and tallest) is photographed from eye height at 3 and 7 m from six sides, from under it, from 40 m and shaking from a hit, each also zoomed in on the top junction, with the wind stopped at that tree's strongest gust (`trees_*.png`). It then checks the smooth shading (needles, bark and clouds), shows every weak spot on every pine and checks that no corner of the X is inside the bark (measuring the old placement too, for comparison) and photographs the worst old spots from the front and the sides, zoomed (`treex_*.png`), and hits a couple of trees and counts, colours, times and photographs the falling leaves (`leaves_*.png`). Add `-trees-only` to run just the trees, the bush, flag, clouds (and one cloud zoomed in), hills and wheat pictures (`close_*.png`: a tall grass patch from outside, the side and above, from its rim looking in, and from inside it with and without the fade).
  - **The map's glass dome** (every map; `MapDome.cs`): where the castle walls and then the grey boundary walls used to be, a **big glass dome covers the whole map**, in the same glass and frame lines as the glass wall that drops. The map is square, so it's a rounded square: at the ground it hugs the edge (a superellipse just outside where the old walls stopped you, so the area you play in is the same - only the very tips of the corners are cut off), its wall goes straight up to a tall shoulder (at least 22 m, and 15 m above the ground anywhere along the edge - Highlands and the theme maps rise there), then curves over and **closes at the top** in a peak in the middle (rounder as it goes up), 48-68 m high: under the clouds and the sun, over the ball drop (40 m). The frame: a **trim line all the way round the bottom, where the glass meets the ground** (it follows the ground, one even band), bands every 6 m up the straight sides and round every third ring above, 20 ribs up to a thicker **crown band** near the top, one more band above it, and 8 ribs on over the top to a little ring round the peak, so the cap reads as closed glass (a bit less detail than before: about a fifth fewer lines, not counting the trim). The mountains, clouds and sun show through it. **Nothing gets out**: it's solid (one mesh collider, faces pointing in) - jetpacks, ender pearls, thrown and shot things all stop at the glass - and the old boundary walls are still there as invisible colliders. The glass walls between the bases fill its cross-section. The airdrop ship hovers over it, cuts a hole in it for the crate and patches it again before it leaves (above): the hole is a see-through patch in the glass and frame (their UVs are the map's x, z and a small mask texture, redrawn only while a hole opens or closes), so the dome is still one draw for the glass and one for the frame. Its colour is **Map dome (glass)** in Settings > Display (it tints the frame and the glass). PSX and AI PSX keep their concrete walls and don't draw the dome (it still stops you).
  - **The sun**: a stylised low-poly sun where the sunlight comes from - a faceted disc in two yellows round a pale middle, a darker rim, a ring of pointed rays and a soft two-step halo, in flat colours (`SkySun` in `SkySun.cs`, unlit shader `Assets/Game/Resources/World/Sun.shader`).   - **How the colour settings reach the models** (`WorldColors.cs`): every colour is a named slot (default, saved colour, swatches). Most models are built from plain colour materials (`Art.Mat`, cached by colour); each model's builder wraps itself in `using (ColorSlots.Use(ColorSlots.Workbench))`, and every `Art.Mat` inside it gets a material of that slot's own, coloured `colour x (picked / default)`. Changing a slot only re-colours those few shared materials (nothing is rebuilt, nothing runs per frame), so all of a model's parts shift together and its main colour lands exactly on the one picked. The grass, wheat and flowers (shader globals), pine needles, clouds, sky, sun, ground and the hands read their slot directly. The grass thins out over the last few metres before the rock (`MapBuilder.RockField`, read by `GrassField`), so it fades into the faces instead of stopping at a line, and none grows on the rock.

## Spears
- Hold RMB to ready the spear and click LMB to throw (a longer wind-up throws harder). Let go of RMB and it's a normal spear again. The throw is timed (minimum wind-up, buffered clicks, a release that can't be cancelled, recovery - see Controls; all in CHANGE VALUES > Spear).
- Thrown spears fly in an arc and stay in the world. Look at one and press **E** to pick it up.
- A spear that hits a player (up to 90 damage at full wind-up; animals take the same) sticks in them. Anyone can press **E** on them to pull it out and keep it, including the victim. Spears stuck in you go into your loot bag if you die.

## Building
- You can only build inside your own base (the tinted square with the grid while the wall is up; once the build phase is over, by default the tinted floor and its grid go and the base is plain flat ground - grass colour, no blades - inside its team-colour border, which stays; Settings > Display > Base floor can keep the grid or grow grass blades there instead, and pick its colours; `MapBuilder.SetGlassWall`). Pieces snap to a 3 m grid. There's no delay between placements, and you can place pieces right where you stand (you get lifted on top).
- Pieces: Foundation (15 wood), Wall (15), Doorway with a lockable door (20), Window (15), Floor (12), Stairs (20).
- You don't have to aim at the bottom edge: look along where you want the wall (eye height or above works) and it goes to the farthest spot along your aim where it can actually go (so it lands where you're pointing).
- **Building in a line**: most people carry on from the piece they just put down, so the ghost leans towards slots that extend **your last two pieces** (foundations and floors next to them - straight on in the line the two make first - walls carrying the wall line on, round a corner or up a level, walls on a foundation's / floor's sides, floors on a wall). It's a bias, not an override: such a slot wins only when your aim is within 1.6 m of it and no more than 0.75 m farther from it than the slot you'd otherwise get (so from the middle of the next foundation a wall carries your wall line on, but aiming right by another edge or at the middle of another cell still goes there). Only your own last two pieces that are still standing count (`BuildGrid.PreferRecent`, `PlayerController.PreferRecentSlot`; `-autotest buildbias`).
- **High external walls** (500 HP, like Rust's): a free-standing palisade of sharpened logs 4 m wide and about 5.5 m tall. Place them out on the map or inside your own base - never in the enemy base (no end of the wall may poke into it) and never on or right next to the enemy's building pieces (in every mode, Builder too), and not on the bedrock.
- Rust-style support: walls and stairs need a foundation or floor, and floors need a wall below or a neighbouring floor. Destroying support collapses what's on it.
- Floors (ceilings) can be built off the top of a ramp (stairs) in every mode, and the floor ghost looks at every level your aim crosses - so from low on the stairs you already see the ceiling go green where you point.
- Upgrade to stone (30-50 stone) for 3-4x HP. Melee does only 20% damage to stone, so bring a ram.
- **No wall straight back where one was just broken**: when a wall, doorway or window is broken down, nobody (either team) can build a wall-type piece in that same spot for **4 s** (Wall Rebuild Cooldown, CHANGE VALUES > Building) - the server refuses it and says "A wall was just broken here - you can build here again in N s" (`Structure.RebuildWait`, checked in `PlayerNet.PlaceRpc`). Demolishing your own doesn't count.
- **Doors sound like your base**: opening and shutting a door plays a sound for the base's current tier - its own tier, or the team's fortify level if that's higher (`Structure.DoorSoundTier`): **wood** creaks open on dry hinges and shuts with a hollow knock, **stone** grinds open and shuts with a heavy thump, **metal** squeals and shuts with a ringing clang, **refined** clicks its lock back, hisses open and shuts with a deep armoured clunk and the bolt shooting home. Everyone near it hears it (played on every screen when the door's state changes; synthesised in `Sfx.MakeDoorSounds` - drop `doorwoodopen_0.ogg` etc. in Resources/Sfx to replace them).
- **Picking things back up**: hold E on an **empty** chest or workbench of your team's (0.8 s, Pack Up Hold Time; a bar fills under the crosshair) and it goes back in your bag (`PlayerNet.PackUpRpc`); a tap still opens a chest.

## Horses and the car
- **Wild horses** wander each half of the map. Craft a **saddle** (it's in your team colour), walk up to one and press E to saddle it and get on. It rides like a Minecraft horse: it goes where you look, Shift gallops, Space jumps. Your view sits high up so you can see ahead.
- **The ball on horseback**: you can get on a horse while carrying the ball, and pick the ball up from the saddle (look at it and press E - E only gets you off when you're not looking at the ball or anything else to loot: **dropped items, chests and bags come first**, so you can grab loot without leaving the saddle; from up there it reaches a little further down for items). LMB throws it from the horse; it keeps the horse's speed. (Cars still can't carry the ball.)
- **Wild Unicorns**: each wild horse spot has a small chance (**Unicorn Chance**, 5%; rolled once per spot, so every half gets one) of being a unicorn instead - white, with a gold horn and hooves and a pastel mane, called **Wild Unicorn** (Saddled Unicorn once saddled) when you look at it. It's a little faster (**Unicorn Speed Mul** x1.15, ridden or fleeing) and tougher (**Unicorn Hp Mul** x1.35, so 81 HP), and **galloping it leaves a rainbow** hanging in the air behind it (see-through: `Vehicle.RainbowAlpha` 0.4). It's synced (`Vehicle.Unicorn`), so everyone sees the same unicorn; it keeps its own model in PSX graphics. Code: `Vehicle.Unicorn.cs`.
- Ridden horses **bob and rock with their stride** (at a walk the bounce is 2.2 times as quick as it was - `Vehicle.BounceMul`; the gallop keeps its own time; a wandering wild horse hops 2.2 times as often too) (more at a gallop; the rider bobs a little with them) and their tail lifts as they run. **Wild horses and unicorns** (nobody on them) **hop about like the tree disguise** as they wander or bolt: a hop every ~2.2 m, up off the ground stretched long, a squash as they land, nose up on take-off and down on landing, settling back to standing when they stop. It's driven by how fast each one really moves, so every screen sees the same (`Vehicle.WildHop`).
- Horses have 60 HP, and a hit on the **head** does double damage, and **shots hit wherever the horse is drawn** - body, neck, mane, muzzle, legs, tail, saddle (hit boxes on its parts, on the players' hitbox layer so they never bump anything; `Vehicle.CreateVisual`; arrows used to go through its neck, chest and rump; your own arrows pass through the horse you ride) (their HP shows when you look at one once it's been hurt), bleed when hit and bolt away from whoever hurt them. **Every weapon hurts them**: melee (rock, tools, sword, spear), arrows and crossbow bolts, thrown spears, every gun (the shotgun's pellets too), the sniper and the death wand (both kill them), and the blast of C4, rockets, airstrikes and the fake bomb bush - all decided on the server. They can be killed: they drop **horse meat** (takes 3 s to eat, heals you fully) and their saddle.
- **Feeding a horse**: hold **berries** and **LMB** on a hurt horse - it eats one berry and gets **20 HP** back (Horse Berry Heal in CHANGE VALUES > Vehicles), up to full; green puffs and a munch show it, and its HP shows while you look at it holding berries. You don't eat the berry yourself (RMB still eats). The server checks you're close, holding berries and that it isn't at full health already (`PlayerNet.FeedHorseRpc`).

## Combat & raid balance
**Explosions** (C4, rockets, the airstrike's bombs, a fake bomb bush - `Fx.Explosion` / `FxBlast` in `Fx.cs`, local on every peer): a bright flash, a **fireball** (a white-hot core and orange lobes, glowing additive balls ~9 m across that swell, rise and burn out), a **column of dark smoke** rolling up and spreading at the top for several seconds, **debris** (chunks of wood, stone and charred bits flying out and landing) and **burning bits trailing smoke**, a **shockwave** ring racing 18 m out along the ground with a pale shell of air and a ring of dust behind it, a **scorch mark** left on the ground (fades in 30 s), and the **camera shakes by how close you are** (hard right on top of it, a nudge out to ~70 m; further off a small rumble when the sound gets to you). **Heard right across the map, like in Rust**: close up a sharp crack and heavy boom; further off a deep rolling boom (`Sfx.FarBoom`, with echoes) that arrives late - sound at 343 m/s, so ~0.7 s from 250 m - and more muffled (low-passed from 5 kHz down to ~380 Hz at 380 m+) and quieter with distance but never silent (`Fx.BoomDelay`, `BoomCutoff`, `BoomVolume`). Several at once (the airstrike's carpet of bombs) get the full show for the first two only and one far-off boom, so it stays cheap. `-autotest victory` checks a close one (fireball, smoke column, hard shake: `victory_explosion_fireball`, `victory_explosion_smoke`) and a far one (late, muffled boom, no shake).

**Gunfire** (`Fx.Gunshot` in `Fx.cs`, local on every peer): the pistol, revolver, shotgun and sniper each have their own layered shot (synthesised in `Sfx.MakeGunSounds`) and a muzzle flash (`FxMuzzle`: a short light, a ball of flame, sparks, a wisp of smoke). Like the explosions it is **heard right across the map**: close by the full crack (to 260 m); from 70 m a dull pop (`Sfx.FarShot`) takes over, arriving late (343 m/s), more muffled (low-passed) and quieter with distance but never silent. Firing feels heavier on your own screen: each gun has its own view-model recoil that snaps and springs back (`ViewModel.Recoil`), a camera kick, a small FOV punch and shake; the rocket launcher lurches back with a burst of flame and backblast smoke, the death wand flicks out with a crackling bolt, a deep boom and a green flash at both ends, the portal gun gives a rubbery thoomp and a pulse of the portal's colour. None of it changes damage, spread or fire rate.

- **You can break your own pieces** too - walls, doors, foundations, high external walls, chests: anything an enemy can break, with the same damage (melee, arrows, thrown spears, bullets, the sniper, the ram and explosives). Friendly fire on players is unchanged.

Drops only settle on things that stay put: they used to stick to a hurt horse's collider where it was in the world, so when it bolted they were left **hanging in mid-air** - now anything that moves (horses, players, the ball, a swinging door, physics bodies) they fall straight past, onto the ground (`FxParticle.Moves`; the same for leaves).

## Project layout
