# Rock Base Brawl

A 1v1 first-person "gather, build, raid" game for Unity 6 (6000.0.42f1, URP) using **Netcode for GameObjects 2.4**.
Both players start with a rock, gather wood and stone, build a Rust-style base, craft tools and weapons, and fight over a ball that waits under a glass dome in the middle of the map.

## How to test

### Option A: two players inside the editor (Multiplayer Play Mode)
1. Open the project and open `Assets/Scenes/Game.unity`.
2. Open **Window > Multiplayer > Multiplayer Play Mode** and tick **Player 2**. Wait for it to boot.
3. Press Play. In the main editor window click **HOST GAME**. In the Player 2 window click **JOIN GAME** (IP `127.0.0.1`).

### Option B: standalone builds (best for LAN play with a friend)
1. **Rock Game > Build Windows Player**. The build goes to `Builds/Windows/RockBaseBrawl.exe`.
2. Run it twice (or on two PCs). One clicks **HOST GAME**, the other types the host's IP and clicks **JOIN GAME**.
   Port 7777/UDP must be reachable; allow the Windows firewall prompt.
   You can also press Play in the editor and host or join from there against a build.

### Main menu
The title at the top reads **ALIEN ROCK GAME** (`Hud.MainTitle`). Laid out top to bottom: **MATCH SETUP** (what the host picks), then **PLAY** (**HOST GAME**, or type the host's IP and **JOIN GAME**; the **port ▼** button next to the IP folds out the port if you need to change it; under JOIN GAME, **SOLO TEST** hosts a match on your own that starts straight away with no opponent - HOST GAME and the old Solo test toggle in one, so there's no Testing row any more), then **SETTINGS** and **QUIT**. The option you've picked shows pressed in with the accent colour (gold to start with; Settings > Display > Interface). Buttons blip when you point at them and click when you press them; sliders tick as they move. There's no Graphics row (the PSX / AI PSX test looks are hidden, everyone plays Normal - `GameSettings.ShowGraphicsPicker` brings the picker back; `-psx` / `-aipsx` still work for the tests) and no Materials row (wood is the normal game now, see below).

### Match setup (set by the host)
- **Players** (one button that folds open the eight choices): red vs blue in *1v1*, *2v2*, *3v3* or *4v4*; or the X-shaped map with 3 or 4 bases (glass walls in an X, the map laid out four ways round): *FFA 3* / *FFA 4* (free for all, one player per base), *2v2v2* or *2v2v2v2* (teams of two). Sudden death is last team standing.
- **Game mode** - separate ways to play; they don't mix. The menu shows **Tutorial**, **Classic** and **Primitive**; the rest fold out under **More modes ▼** (Arsenal, Builder, Primitive Limited, Building Primitive, DNA, Fun mode, Random Fun, Random Fun Limited; the button shows the one picked). Renamed on request: **Classic is now the Auto Wood rules** (below), the original game is now called **Primitive**, and the old Primitive is **Primitive Limited**. Only the names changed: the synced `GameRules` values and the `-rules` names on the command line are the old ones (`-rules classic` = the original game, the menu's Primitive; `-rules autowood` = the menu's Classic; `-rules primitive` = Primitive Limited; new aliases `original`, `primitivelimited`, `limited`). A small panel on the left gives one simple step at a time - a title, a sentence or two and a goal - and you have to actually **do** each one to move on (there's no skipping). **The game is revealed a bit at a time: nothing works until its step teaches it.** At the start you can only look around with the mouse; WASD unlocks on the walk step, Shift and Space on run-and-jump, Ctrl on crouch, C on slide, LMB (and the hotbar strip) on the first chop, TAB on the bag step, the crafting list on the first craft (and it only lists what's been taught so far: hatchet, then building plan, spear, workbench, the workbench tiers - the bow, arrows, chest and ram once you've finished), the number keys / mouse wheel on the axe step, placing pieces on the floor step, RMB (the building wheel) on the wall step, E on the door step, X on the break-your-wall step, the spear throw, the health bar on the food step, and putting things down and the workbench lists on the workbench steps at the end. Pressing something that's still locked does nothing and the panel says "Not yet!"; each step's panel lists what it just unlocked ("NEW: ..."). **Nothing in it needs or mentions stone** (no stone, pickaxe, mining or ram steps; everything it has you craft costs wood only). The steps (30): look at your machine, walk, run and jump, crouch (Ctrl), slide (C), chop a tree, hit the X, get 100 wood, open your bag, craft a hatchet, chop with it (pick it with its number key), go home, craft the building plan, place a foundation, a wall (the building wheel) and a doorway, **break your own wall** (X, or just hit it), craft a spear, throw it and pick it up, pick a berry and eat it (the step makes you hungry first), then **the glass wall**: walk up to it (the ball waits under the glass dome in the middle, where the walls meet), it drops with the dome, run to the ball, grab it, throw it into your machine (**that captures it, which is what unlocks the workbench** - like in a match) and guard it (enemies can steal it), then **the workbench**: "WORK BENCHES UNLOCKED" (the notice waits for this step), craft a Workbench T1, put it down anywhere in your base, open your bag in your base and see the new WORKBENCH T1 list (the step also tells you about the Workbench T2 and scrolling the list). The finish card mentions the ball buff, the other starter items (bow, arrows, chests, the ram), airdrops (the 15 second warning), UPGRADES at the upgrade station (green plus, left of your machine) in Classic / Arsenal (the tutorial itself has the original rules, so no upgrades), feeding hurt horses berries, and Settings > Display (shadows, post processing, the UI's font and size, the grass and colours). A pulsing marker points at what the step is about (at a tree when you're short of wood) - **only ever a tree or bush in your own team's part of the map** (`Tutorial.Nearest`; it never sends you over to the enemy's side). The "more wood" step mentions the fallen logs (wood too, with an X) and that a felled tree grows back. If a step takes over 40 s a one-line hint shows up. While the inventory is open the goal shows along the bottom.
    - **Multiplayer**: friends can join a tutorial any time (up to the players picked) - the match starts as soon as the host is in, and anyone who joins later is dropped straight into their base. Everyone goes through the steps at their own pace. The glass wall drops **once every player has walked up to it** (the others see "waiting for N more players"), or 90 s after the first one got there so nobody's stuck waiting for someone who wandered off; nobody wins by the others leaving. There's one ball: whoever gets it first captures it for their team; the others take it out of that machine (E) and capture it for their own team (each team's workbench unlocks when it captures the ball). Each player's locks follow their own step: `Tutorial.Allows(TutFeature)` / `AllowsItem` on their machine (checked in `Binds` for every key, `PlayerController` and the HUD), and the server checks crafting (including the workbench tiers), building and putting things down against `PlayerNet.TutStep` (each client writes its step there). Outside the tutorial every gate is always open. Each log rolls out of a lit port, down the chute (held up off the ground on a strut), **drops off the end** and lands on the pile 1.65 m out in front of the machine (`Cfg.WoodTrayLocal`), so you can see them fall out and the pile is never in the machine. Walk up and press E to take the lot: the pile keeps stacking past 1000 and splits into 1000-stacks as it goes into your inventory. **Wood Gen upgrade** (in **UPGRADES** - press E on your **upgrade station**, left of the alien machine; Auto Wood only, there from the start), two levels: level 1 for 1000 wood (12 a second), level 2 for 3000 (25 a second) - all in CHANGE VALUES > Auto Wood. The wood machine is **futuristic tech in the alien machine's style**, all low - **nothing stands on top of it** (no column, rings, orb or pylons; the top is a finished metal lid with a silver panel outlined in the level colour and flat louvres): a silver housing on a metal plinth, a console with a screen on one side and a spinning saw blade on the other (sawdust flies off it). It changes in front of you with each upgrade: **level 0** is silver with green trim and a team-colour and a green lens in its lid; **level 1** is bigger and goes **yellow** - yellow light strips up every edge and round the base, yellow and black hazard stripes on the lid and the chute, and two exhaust vents low on the back puffing yellow; **level 2** is bigger again and goes **pink** - it hovers over a pink glow on four short tesla legs, pink seams down its sides, a pink diamond and studs on the lid, a second saw and four vents puffing pink (lit pips on its front show the level). Code in `WoodMachine.cs`; the `-autotest modes -rules autowood` run photographs every level next to the alien machine (`autowood_machine_N`), close up (`autowood_machine_close_N`) and a log caught dropping off the chute onto the pile (`autowood_log_landing_N`).

  - *Building Primitive*: Builder's rules (below), but only those four primitive items and no power items.
  - *DNA*: the classic game, but **trees and rocks give DNA** (a glowing double helix) instead of wood and stone - rocks give 1.5x as much per hit, and there are fewer than half as many of them. **Everything is bought with DNA** at the same ratios: wood prices are DNA 1:1, every stone becomes 1.5 DNA (crafting, building, upgrading, refunds; airdrop wood/stone comes as DNA). Every base has a **gambling machine** on the bedrock to the left of the alien machine: a slot machine with a lever, three reels and a payout hatch. E opens it like a chest with 3 slots that only take DNA (your bet) and a big **GAMBLE** button under them. Press it and the menu closes: the lever drops and the reels spin on the machine (everyone sees it), landing one by one. 50/50 (Gamble Win Chance): three DNA = confetti, a jingle, JACKPOT on your screen and **double your bet** spat out of the hatch; anything else = the screen goes red with X's, a loud buzzer, the cabinet shakes, a voice says "LOSER" and a bouncing **YOU LOST** fills your screen. The numbers are in CHANGE VALUES > DNA mode. (The "LOSER" voice is `Assets/Game/Resources/Gamble/loser.wav`, made with Windows' speech synthesiser.)
  - *Builder*: **no bases, no machines**. Arsenal's items and prices, but most crafts take a few seconds: small timers stack up in the top right: the top one is being made (its bar runs down), and anything else you buy meanwhile waits under it with a full bar, moving up and starting when its turn comes (like Rust - one thing at a time, paid for up front). The building plan and Fortify All Walls are instant; you can **craft, build and put chests anywhere**; pieces **lock on to each other** Fortnite style (walls hang off the walls they touch, floors and stairs hold on to walls around them, ground-level walls reach down into the ground). **The ball** can't be thrown: anyone can pick it up, and **LMB (or E) puts it down** right in front of you - a block in your team's colour grows up under it and it's your team's ball. The ball always has a flag pointing at the sky (white when loose, the colour of whoever has it); set **Builder Flag Always Up** to Off in CHANGE VALUES to go back to a flag that only grows out while the ball is planted. When anyone picks the ball up, the block sinks away again. Whoever's ball it is (planted, not carried) when time runs out wins; otherwise it's sudden death.
  - *Fun mode*: no building phase - the wall is down and the ball is in from the start - and a 10 minute match. Everybody gets the **same** random item straight away and then every 45 s (any item in the game, the unused ones too). Free items land on the right of your hotbar, like wood does (then the inventory). No airdrops in the Fun modes.
  - *Random Fun*: the same, but only airdrop items (the unused ones too) and everyone gets a **different** one.
  - *Random Fun Limited*: Random Fun, but you can only craft the hatchet, spear, building plan and battering ram.
  - In both Fun modes the **Game length** row changes to the match length and the free item interval (both - / +).

  - **First person**: the alien's own forearms and clawed hands (cut from the player model by `Tools/psx_convert.py`), tinted in your team colour like your body (or the colour picked in Settings > Display > World colours > First-person hands), in every graphics mode. (The pack's first-person arms are human, so they aren't used.) Each hand sits on the same hand transform as the old block fist and follows all its animations, but the held poses copy where Rust holds each item once it's pulled out (studied frame by frame from Rust's equip animations): the **rock** sits cupped low in the middle of the screen in both palms, the claws up its sides (placed by the size of whichever rock shows, so the PSX slab is held too); the **hatchet, pickaxe, sword and tree cracker** are one fist round the end of the handle at the bottom right, the back of the fist towards you, the handle upright with the head a little forward and the blade to the left (a swing turns it to face forward first); the **spear** is one fist low on the right with the tip up and forward towards the middle (a thrust levels it); the **crossbow and guns** have the trigger fist square round the pistol grip, and a long gun's fore-end rests in the other palm with the claws up its far side, while the pistol, revolver and portal gun are one-handed like Rust's (the other hand comes up only to reload); the **building plan** is held by its bottom edge and leans away from you, its rolled-up top on the far side. The hands are 1.65x the model's size, about as broad as a person's next to the items, so a fist covers a handle. The forearms bend at the **wrist**: each runs from the wrist towards an elbow below the edge of the screen (`RightElbow` / `LeftElbow`, up to 75 degrees off straight), so an arm always comes up into its hand from the bottom of the screen while the fist sits square round the handle (a slanted handle stops the claws closing). The model's claws are over twice the palm's length, far too long to close round a handle, so they're shortened to about two thirds (the thumb to 82%). Every frame something moves relative to the hand, the claws close onto what's actually held (`ViewModel.Hands.cs`): the held item's meshes - whichever look is showing, the PSX models' pieces in PSX graphics - are cut through along each digit's own plane (in thin strips across its width), the palm is put onto the item (against a handle with the knuckles just past it, or onto the surface in front of an open hand, under something cradled), then each claw closes with all its joints together, a segment that touches stopping along with the joints before it while the rest carry on curling round, so it wraps the real shape and ends about 2 mm above its surface; a segment that starts inside something bends back out first. In a fist the thumb wraps round the handle alongside the claws (or, where there's no room, closes in towards them); nothing curls into the palm. A fist that can't close cleanly (a gun's body right over its grip) slides along the handle to where it can. Tools, the spear, the bow's grip, the guns' grips, the death wand and the arrow get fists; food, potions, C4, chests, armour, the helmet and the like are cradled on the palm with the claws up their sides; the rock, the ball, the ram's log, the long guns' fore-ends, a pistol's side while reloading, the building plan's bottom edge and the chainsaw's top handle get palms on the surface with the claws round it; the bowstring is hooked just behind the arrow's nock. A grip once worked out is remembered, so picking the same thing up again costs nothing. `-autotest hands -host -solo -shotdir DIR` (add `-hands rock_,bow_` to do only the poses starting with those, `-handsclose` for close-ups of each hand from outside, in front and below) photographs every item and the key moments of each animation (swing raised / slam, bow half / fully drawn, spear wind-up, crossbow aim, eating, throwing, the ball) with the old block arms, the alien arms and both together, plus a side view of each, and measures every pose: each rigid piece of the hand (palm, forearm, each claw and thumb segment) and each piece of the item become temporary convex colliders, and it logs the deepest overlap, the hand against itself and each digit's gap to the surface (`hands_contact.csv`, `grip_<pose>.json` with the solver's outlines); it fails if anything sinks in more than 4 mm. **Dead players** are a skeleton on the ground (the PSX dead model). **Hits** leave PSX blood splats on the ground for 30 s.
  - Item icons always show the Normal look.
  - The models are made from the pack by `Tools/psx_convert.py` (Blender, headless): each one is joined, turned to the game's conventions, centred, its textures shrunk to at most 256 px, and exported to `Assets/Game/Resources/PsxModels/` with `psx_manifest.txt` (each model's textures by material slot). The texture packs are 3x3 atlases, cut into `tex_<pack>_<row><col>.png` tiles. `PsxModels.cs` swaps them in, fitted into the space the Normal look takes up. `-autotest psxmodels -host -solo` photographs every item next to its Normal look, the world, building pieces and first person, then checks Normal comes back exactly.

Code in `AiPsxArt.cs`. `-autotest aipsx -aipsx` takes pictures of it and checks Normal and PSX come back exactly as they were.

**Explosives against fortified pieces**: wood and stone go as usual. **C4** on sheet metal only blows out that layer: the piece it's stuck to and the ones right next to it on the same side (not the walls behind it, and not floors under a wall); on refined it only breaks the one piece it's stuck to. **Rockets** do 45% to sheet metal and 20% to refined (Metal / Refined Rocket Mul). An **airstrike** flattens wood and stone and knocks sheet metal and refined down a step.

While you hold the revolver or the shotgun, the rounds loaded and your spare ammo show big in the top left. (The pistol isn't sold any more; it's still in the Fun mode item pool.)

All these numbers are in CHANGE VALUES under "Arsenal and Builder" (and "Fun modes" for the item interval and Fun match length).

## Display settings code (COPY / PASTE SETTINGS)
Settings > Display > **COPY SETTINGS** gives a code like this (one `key = value` a line; `#` starts a comment, except the `#` a colour starts with):
```
ALIEN ROCK GAME display settings v1
# Settings > Display (everything except the screen settings), 2026-10-04 12:00
# One "key = value" per line. Paste it back with PASTE SETTINGS; unknown keys are ignored, missing ones are left as they are.
# ---- POST PROCESSING ----
post = on
post.bloom = on
post.bloom.strength = 0.65    # changed (default 0.5)
post.extra.outlines = on    # changed (default off)
post.extra.outlines.strength = 0.5
# ---- POST PROCESSING ON THE UI ----
ui.post = on    # changed (default off)
ui.post.world = on
ui.cel = on    # changed (default off)
ui.outline.width = 4    # changed (default 2)
ui.outline.colour = #000000
# ---- SHADOWS ----
shadows.darkness = 0.8    # changed (default 1)
...
# ---- WORLD COLOURS ----
colour.hands.team = on
colour.Sky = #8FC1E8
...
# 7 values changed from the defaults.
```
- **Values**: switches are `on` / `off` (also `true/false`, `yes/no`, `1/0`); numbers are plain (`.` decimal; a trailing `%` is divided by 100, a unit after the number like `120 m` or `4 px` is ignored) in the same units as the code shows (strengths 0-1, distances in metres, `ui.outline.width` in pixels at 1080p); colours `#RRGGBB`; `ui.font` and `ui.accent` by name (`Bahnschrift`, `Alien green`) or number. Values outside a setting's range are clamped; while Settings > Display is open the sliders snap a value to their steps.
- **Pasting** (`DisplayCode.Apply`): the header line (`ALIEN ROCK GAME display settings v<N>`) isn't required, but without it at least one known key is (so pasting random text is refused, nothing changed). **Unknown keys are ignored** (a code from a newer game still pastes what this one knows), **keys that aren't there keep their current values** (so a code can be just the three lines you want), a value that doesn't parse is skipped; the message counts them. Lines from the old world colours copy (`TreeTrunks = #4F8F2A`, `HandsUseTeamColour = false`) still work.
- **Making a code the game's defaults**: every number / switch default is in **one table**, `DisplayDefaults` at the top of `Assets/Scripts/DisplaySettings.cs` - one `const` per setting, each with its code key in a comment (`public const float BloomStrength = 0.5f; // post.bloom.strength`); the settings' own code (`UiLook.cs`, `PostFx.cs`, `WorldLook.cs`, `Hud.Fps.cs`, `Config.AlienOutline*`, the Defaults buttons) all read from it. The world colours' defaults are the `Add(...)` lines in `WorldColors.cs` (`colour.<Id>` = the slot whose id is `<Id>`). So: give Claude the code and say "make these the defaults" - only the `# changed` lines need moving.
- **Adding a new display setting to the code** (`DisplayCode` in `DisplaySettings.cs`): **one line** in `DisplayCode.Table()`, e.g.
  `Float("beams.strength", "BEAMS", DisplayDefaults.BeamStrength, () => GameSettings.BeamStrength, (v, save) => GameSettings.SetBeams(v, GameSettings.BeamFalloff, save));`
  (`Bool`, `Float`, `Colour`, or `Choice(key, group, names, default, get, set)` for a pick from a list; the group is the `# ---- ... ----` heading it's listed under; the setter gets `save` - pass it on so the tests can change things without saving), plus its default in `DisplayDefaults`. Or declare the setting as a self-saving **`DisplayPref`** field of `GameSettings` - `public static readonly DisplayPref.Float BeamStrength = new("beams.strength", "BEAMS", DisplayDefaults.BeamStrength, 0f, 1f);`, read `.Value`, change it with `.Set(v)` (saved in PlayerPrefs as `RockGame.Disp.<key>`; `DisplayPref.Changed` fires) - and it's in the code **with no registration at all** (`DisplayCode` finds every `DisplayPref` there is; the UI's own looks are done this way, see `UiLook.cs`). A new world colour slot (`ColorSlots.Add`) is in the code automatically too. Keep the key once chosen (it's what old codes use); the screen settings (window, resolution, refresh rate) stay out on purpose.

## Rules
- In the middle of each base is an unbreakable silver **bedrock** (2x2 cells). You spawn on it. It counts as a foundation (walls and stairs can stand on it) but nothing can be placed on it.
- On the bedrock stands your **alien machine** (with room to build walls behind it, and room for chests around it). It has a **socket** (the glowing cradle under the arch) where the ball has to sit.
- **Spears and hatchets can be crafted anywhere; everything else only inside your own base** (TAB).
- A **big glass wall** splits the map in half for the first **5 minutes**. The **ball** is there from the start, sitting in the middle of the map under a **glass dome** (the dome covers the round ball zone; the glass walls don't cross the middle - they meet the dome and run up over its top, so there's no way round or over). Nobody can reach the ball (or pick it up) until the wall drops - then **the wall and the dome slide down into the ground** (a shudder, then faster and faster, easing in at the bottom, about 4 s; dust rolls out along its foot, a crack of glass, a rumble and a thud), the banner says **THE WALL IS DROPPING**, the ball is up for grabs and the match timer starts (15 minutes, Match Length). It counts as down the moment it starts to drop: its colliders go at once (so the ball's free and nobody is stopped by glass on its way down) and hits across it count; every peer starts the slide when its match state turns, so everyone sees the same thing (joining after it dropped, or the Fun modes, it's simply gone). Code: `MapBuilder.DropGlassWall` / `GlassWallDrop`, called from `NetGame.OnStateChanged`. **The emergency flare tip**: while the wall is up (every mode), walk up to the glass wall in the middle (within 7 m of it and 45 m of the middle, or up to the ball's dome) and a card fades in over the ball in the tutorial's marker style (the gold diamond): **EMERGENCY FLARE** / *Have it in your base when the timer ends to win* - the ball is the emergency flare (`FlareTip.cs`, drawn by the HUD). ## Airdrops
Each airdrop gets **one warning, 15 seconds before it lands** (`NetGame.DropWarning`): a banner **AIRDROP IN 15 SECONDS** saying where it's coming down (*"It's dropping in the centre of the map!"* with Where = Middle of the map; every side / somewhere on the map otherwise), plus a line in the chat. There's no airdrop timer on the HUD and no other countdown. Once the wall is down, a giant alien ship comes down from very high up (in over the nearest edge of the map), hovers **over the map's glass dome** (clear of the glass, its open doors too - `AirdropShip.HoverAt`; **70 m up** - it used to be 45 - but never above 88 m for the height alone, so its top stays well under the clouds and nowhere near the Highlands' sky planets; higher only where the dome's glass makes it), **opens a round hatch in its belly** (two half-round doors swing down, the hole glows purple), and its **purple beam comes down onto the glass and cuts a round hole in it** (`MapDome.SetHole`: the hole opens out round the beam with its edge glowing purple, and a **rim in the same style as the glass's frame lines** - a wide band and a thin line just outside it - runs round it; the frame lines that crossed it go too). Then the beam carries on down through the hole to a random spot (never close to a base; follow the purple beam) and **beams an airdrop crate** out of the hatch, **down through the hole**. The beam is glowing light (like the ball's beacon, `BeamFx`): a wide purple column, a bright white-violet core and a faint halo round it, with bands of light running down it and a glow on the ground where it lands, so it's easy to spot from across the map - as bright as the ball's beacon from far away, fading down to a faint purple column as you come up to it (Settings > Display > Beams). The ship **shines light down out of its belly** all the time it's there (a real spotlight onto the ground under it, brighter with the hatch open, a soft purple glow under the hull and a ring of glowing lights round its underside), and **a soft light under it shines back up at the hull**, so its underside is lit and doesn't melt into the sky. The crate comes down the beam at **half the old speed** (8 s; the crate lands 13 s after the ship appears): it's the real crate's **solid** look (not a see-through ghost), lit by a little white light on the side you're looking from, and **the beam goes see-through round it** (the shader's `_Clear` points), so you can watch it come down inside the light. Once it's landed its beacon is a beam too (`Container.CrateBeacon`). Once it's down and the beam is off, **the hole is patched** (it closes back up, its edge glowing, in about 1.5 s), the hatch shuts, and **only then** does the ship **fly off over the edge of the map, shrinking smoothly into the distance** until it's gone (no popping out). Its whole path stays low: it comes in from about 110 m over its hover point and leaves almost level, under the clouds (never above 98 m, `AirdropShip.LeaveCeiling`), well clear of anything far off in the sky. *Why it used to pop out:* (1) it climbed 60 m as it left - right up into the cloud layer (105-160 m, clouds 2-4x size) - so from far away, where you look at it nearly sideways through the clouds, a cloud swallowed it while it was still big; (2) each lane had one ship picture, torn down and rebuilt whenever the lane started its next drop, so a drop launched within ~9 s of the last one landing (several airdrops per match, fast timers, the dev button) deleted the leaving ship on the spot. Now every flight is its own object that finishes its exit even after its lane has moved on (each with its own hole in the dome). It's all driven by the drop's synced start time, so the host and every client see the same hole at the same moment. The dome's collider stays whole while the hole is open (the crate coming down is only a picture - the real one appears on the ground when the beam gets there), so nothing can get out through it. How many come per match and which items they can have are set in **MODE OPTIONS**, and how common each one is in **CHANGE VALUES > Airdrop rarity**; a crate nobody emptied stays put. ## Game settings (CHANGE VALUES)
**CHANGE VALUES** on the main menu lists every stat: timings, airdrops, player HP and speeds, damage and gather rate for every tool, node HP, building costs and HP, crafting costs and more. They're folded into sections: click a heading to open it (each shows how many values it has and how many you changed). **Search** (the box at the top, **x** clears it): type and the list keeps only the values whose name or section has every word you typed (any case, spaces optional - `slide`, `bow speed`, `airdrop`), with the sections that have a match open under their heading ("8 of 27") and the matching words in gold; it scrolls back to the top as you type and the mouse wheel scrolls the results. Only the values you change are saved on your PC (so new defaults still reach you). When you host, your values are used for the whole match and sent to everyone.

- **Airdrop rarity** is **not in CHANGE VALUES any more: it's on the MODE OPTIONS screen**, as each item's drop chance with - / + (`Hud.IsModeOptionsSection`). It's still a weight for every airdrop item (Rarity C4, Rarity Death Wand, ... all 10 to start with, still host values sent to everyone and in the exported file): an item with 20 comes twice as often as one with 10, 0 never comes. Only the items ticked in MODE OPTIONS are in the draw (if they're all 0 they're equally likely). Used by airdrop crates, the respawn-loot option and Fun Random (`Cfg.PickAirdropItem`, `Cfg.AirdropChance`).
- **Export to a file** writes `RockBaseBrawl-settings.txt` next to the game (the project folder in the editor): every value, plus the main menu choices and mode options, with the changed ones marked. Send it over and say what it's for - new default settings, or the base of a new game mode.
- **Import from the file** reads it back in; **Open the folder** shows where it is.

## Inventory
- Wood, stone, arrows and berries are real items that take up slots. Tools and weapons go to the hotbar first.
- Materials stack onto what you already have; otherwise they go on the hotbar from **slot 6 (the last) backwards** (never into the empty slot you're holding your rock in), then the main inventory.
- Everything that goes into your inventory pops up in the bottom right ("+30 Wood"); materials spent on building and crafting show up there in red ("-15 Wood").
- Chests and high external walls can go right up against your walls and the machine: if the exact spot is a little too tight they slide into the nearest free spot (up to 0.7 m).
- A chest you open shows up to the **right** of your inventory.
- You always hold something: the hotbar only scrolls through slots that have an item. The rock stays on your hotbar - it can't be dropped or put in a chest.
- **Drag an item outside the inventory** to throw it on the ground; look at it and press E to pick it up.
- **Six across**: the hotbar (6, keys 1-6), the bag (3 rows of 6) and chests (2 rows of 6), with bigger slots than the old seven-wide grid. (`Cfg.HotbarSize` / `MainSize` / `ChestSlots`: 6 / 18 / 12, so 24 player slots on the network.)
- Drag to move or swap, **right-drag** to split a stack in half, **shift-click** to quick-move (between chest/bag and inventory, or hotbar and inventory). **Shift+drag** quick-moves every slot the pointer passes over (each slot once per drag, so you can sweep your whole bag into a chest; fast moves don't skip slots, and something that just moved into a slot isn't carried back). Under the bag it always says just, in white: *Drag to move* / *Right+Drag to Split* / *Shift+Click/Drag to quick-move*. A dragged item is just its icon on the pointer (no slot background behind it).
- **Items fly to their slot**: whatever moves (a drag, a split, shift-click into or out of a chest, a chest sorting itself) flies from where it was - or from the pointer, when you dragged it - to its new slot in a quick arc (0.24 s) and pops as it lands (a quick 0.08 s pop) (`Hud.Flights.cs`: worked out on your screen by comparing the slots with the frame before, so the server's rules don't change).
- **Right-drag shows the half on the pointer** from the moment you pick it up (and the slot keeps the other half) while you choose where it goes.
- **Point at anything** (an item in the bag or chest, or a crafting row) and its **name** (white) and **description** (grey) show under the crafting list (under the chest when one is open).
- **Shift-clicking things into a chest sorts it**: stacks of the same thing are topped up and everything is laid out in order - wood and stone, ammo, weapons, tools, things you place, armour, food, then the rest. (Dragging things in by hand leaves them where you put them.)
- **Storage chest**: craft it (TAB), hold it and click to place it anywhere inside your base. Press E on it to open it next to your inventory (12 slots: 2 rows of 6). Enemies who break in can loot it, and breaking it spills its contents into a bag. C4 only breaks one it's stuck right onto (not through a wall); rockets, the ram and melee break it as before.

## Gathering
- Normal graphics: Plains and Highlands are a thick, stylised meadow - tall blades of grass, dark at the root and bright at the tip, that sway in the wind and part round players, horses, chests, the ball and dropped items; patches of **tall golden wheat** (fewer of them than there were, further apart; each one has a knee-high rim of longer, green-gold grass round it and then **a clear step up to the tall middle** - head high and more, about 1.3 to 3 m, tallest in the very middle - so you can see where the tall grass starts; you can hide in it standing or crouching; it only parts a little as you push through, and there's none near the bases or the ball zone. How: the patches are the tops of a slow noise; the grass field stores how far in from its patch's edge each spot is (a distance transform), and the shader steps the height up a third of the way in (at most 3 m); patches too small to have a tall middle are dropped), clearings of short grass with white daisies, purple (and some pink and white) lupins and clumps of broad leaves - over ground of one flat colour (Highlands' steep rock is one flat grey). No grass on the bases, the ball drop zone, steep rock, or under anything built: **every building piece, chest, workbench and gamble machine keeps the grass off its footprint** (turned with it, out to 0.9 m round it, so no blade pokes through the mesh; not under an upper floor) while it stands, and **the grass grows back when it's destroyed** (`GrassField.Block` / `Unblock`: the grass field's G is worked out again just under the piece that changed, from the field as it was generated, and the texture is uploaded once a frame at most). Fallen logs the same, and the grass round them lies flat.
- **Birds** (`Birds.cs`, every graphics mode): now and then (15% of hits on a tree with no birds in it, at most 2 flocks per team about the map) a hit sends a **flock of 5-9 little dark birds** flapping up out of the tree with a burst of wing flaps and chirps; they fly in an arc to another tree 15-70 m away and settle on its branches (a chirp as they land). **Hit that tree and they fly on to another**, and so on (felled, or flattened by an airstrike, the same). Everyone sees the same flock: the server picks the trees and the flight time and sends one message (from, to, how many, a seed); each player flies the birds along the same paths, and the tree they land in keeps how many sit in it (`ResourceNode.Birds`), so someone joining later sees them too. Each bird is a tiny body and two flapping wings (no shadows).
  - **Inside the tall wheat (or any grass) the blades right at your eyes fade out**: blades closer than ~0.3 m aren't drawn and they fade in (a fine dither pattern, so they stay solid and sorted) out to ~1.7 m - measured with the height counted double, so the grass at your feet stays. **The tall grass in a patch fades much more**: none closer than ~0.8 m, and it only comes back fully at ~3.4 m (staying see-through for longer on the way: that fade is squared). Standing or crouching in the wheat, your screen isn't covered and you can see out round you. It's only round your own camera: everyone else still sees you hidden in it. (`_GrassNearFade`, set from `GrassField.NearFadeFrom / NearFadeTo` and `NearFadeTallFrom / NearFadeTallTo`; `Grass.shader` clips in every pass.)
  - **Trampled grass** (like Rust): wherever players, horses, cars and the ball go, and where dropped items and chests lie, the grass is pressed flat (each blade falls its own way, a little paler) and stands back up by itself: it stays flat for ~4 s after the last step and is fully up again ~15 s later. The tall wheat isn't flattened. How: a "trample map" (one value every 0.5 m over the whole map: when that spot was last stepped on) that the grass shader reads, so nothing has to update while it recovers; ten times a second whatever moved stamps the strip it went over, and only the small squares that changed are copied to the GPU (16x16 blocks, `Graphics.CopyTexture`). Each PC tracks its own (it's only looks).
  - Kept fast (it's tuned on an integrated Intel GPU: about half a millisecond a frame at the default settings): one 8 m patch of blades is drawn on every 8 m square in view near you, in one draw per level of detail (procedural instancing); further out there are fewer, wider blades, single triangles past ~20 m, and past ~85 m the same patch is stretched over 16 m and then (past ~240 m) 32 m squares, so the number of squares stays small even at 270 m; the grass shrinks smoothly into the ground (no popping) and is gone by the render distance picked in Settings > Display (60 m to start with, up to 270 m; the flowers stop at 90 m). The density setting draws only the first share of each patch's blades (they're stored in random order, so it thins evenly) and picks the cheaper patch meshes. Blades are lit per vertex. Where grass grows comes from a 1-texel-a-metre "grass field" texture (height, how much grass, how far into a wheat patch, short grass) that the shader reads, so the same patch follows the hills. Code in `GrassField.cs` and `Assets/Game/Resources/Grass/` (`Grass.shader`). Hidden in PSX and AI PSX.
- Normal graphics, the rest of the world (`WorldDressing.cs`, `WorldLook.cs`, shader `Assets/Game/Resources/World/Painted.shader`: vertex-coloured meshes - the pines and the clouds smooth-shaded (`MeshKit.SmoothNormals`: each corner's normal is the average of the faces meeting there, except across sharp edges) - lit like the grass, with the wind done in the vertex shader):
  - **Pine trees** are tall (10-16 m) and come in **five kinds**, picked from each tree's seed (so every player sees the same tree): *classic* (four even tiers), *spruce* (slim, six narrow bluish tiers), *fir* (full and bushy, five wide flared tiers starting low), *droopy* (bell-shaped tiers whose branch tips hang down) and *scraggly* (sparser, uneven olive tiers higher up an older trunk with a few bare, broken-off branches). Each has a touch of colour of its own. A tier is a star-shaped skirt of branch tips, a ring part way up and the tip, with a shallow cone underneath. Needles and trunk are one mesh (two draws: needles, bark - tinted by the Pine needles / Tree trunks colours), **smooth-shaded** (the light rolls round the trunk and over each tier; a tier's rim over its underside stays a crisp edge), and **sway together** in the same gusts as the grass (taller trees a little more), shaking together when hit.
    - **Every hit on a tree shakes a few leaves loose**: 4-7 little flakes in that tree's own needle colour flutter down from just under its lowest tiers (mostly on your side), swinging side to side and tipping as they drift down, and lie on the ground for a moment before they shrink away (`FxLeaf` in `Fx.cs`; played with the hit's wood chips, so everyone who sees the hit sees them; PSX trees and palms drop them from low in their crown).
    - **The trunk never shows through the needles** (`ResourceNode.PineShape`): inside the crown it's never thicker than the tiers round it can hide (less a margin for the branch tips' flutter), so it tapers up through the crown and stops a little way into the top tier; it's part of the swaying mesh, so the needles can't slide off it in a gust. *Why it used to show:* the trunk was Unity's built-in cylinder, which is 2 units wide (not 1), so it was really 1.2 m thick - twice what the tier layout allowed for - and it stuck out between every pair of tiers (and it stood still while the needles swayed). The trunk is still 1.2 m thick (a flared, ten-sided log) where you hit it, and the collider reaches out to the bark's ten corners and 2 cm past (0.65 m radius, as tall as the trunk inside the needles: the X sits on the corners, so aimed at side on it still has to hit the tree - at 0.6 m the swing grazed past an X on the side of the trunk); the weak spots are where they were. **The X sits on the bark you see, never in it**: it's placed against the trunk mesh's own triangles, facing straight out, as far out as the bark comes anywhere behind it (`ResourceNode.BarkSpot`), worked out in the tree's own space so it shakes with it exactly. *Why it was sometimes inside:* it was put on the round collider, but the trunk has ten flat sides, so wherever a spot faced one of its corners the bark stood out past the collider and the middle of the X was buried in it (and while the tree shook, the collider lagged a frame behind).
    - `-autotest world` checks it for real: every tree's needles and bark are moved on the CPU exactly as the shader moves them (`WorldLook.PaintedWind`) at 16 moments of the wind, and every bit of bark above the lowest underside has to be inside a tier; then one tree of each kind (and the shortest and tallest) is photographed from eye height at 3 and 7 m from six sides, from under it, from 40 m and shaking from a hit, each also zoomed in on the top junction, with the wind stopped at that tree's strongest gust (`trees_*.png`). It then checks the smooth shading (needles, bark and clouds), shows every weak spot on every pine and checks that no corner of the X is inside the bark (measuring the old placement too, for comparison) and photographs the worst old spots from the front and the sides, zoomed (`treex_*.png`), and hits a couple of trees and counts, colours, times and photographs the falling leaves (`leaves_*.png`). Add `-trees-only` to run just the trees, the bush, flag, clouds (and one cloud zoomed in), hills and wheat pictures (`close_*.png`: a tall grass patch from outside, the side and above, from its rim looking in, and from inside it with and without the fade).
  - **The map's glass dome** (every map; `MapDome.cs`): where the castle walls and then the grey boundary walls used to be, a **big glass dome covers the whole map**, in the same glass and frame lines as the glass wall that drops. The map is square, so it's a rounded square: at the ground it hugs the edge (a superellipse just outside where the old walls stopped you, so the area you play in is the same - only the very tips of the corners are cut off), its wall goes straight up to a tall shoulder (at least 22 m, and 15 m above the ground anywhere along the edge - Highlands and the theme maps rise there), then curves over and **closes at the top** in a peak in the middle (rounder as it goes up), 48-68 m high: under the clouds and the sun, over the ball drop (40 m). The frame: a **trim line all the way round the bottom, where the glass meets the ground** (it follows the ground, one even band), bands every 6 m up the straight sides and round every third ring above, 20 ribs up to a thicker **crown band** near the top, one more band above it, and 8 ribs on over the top to a little ring round the peak, so the cap reads as closed glass (a bit less detail than before: about a fifth fewer lines, not counting the trim). The mountains, clouds and sun show through it. **Nothing gets out**: it's solid (one mesh collider, faces pointing in) - jetpacks, ender pearls, thrown and shot things all stop at the glass - and the old boundary walls are still there as invisible colliders. The glass walls between the bases fill its cross-section. The airdrop ship hovers over it, cuts a hole in it for the crate and patches it again before it leaves (above): the hole is a see-through patch in the glass and frame (their UVs are the map's x, z and a small mask texture, redrawn only while a hole opens or closes), so the dome is still one draw for the glass and one for the frame. Its colour is **Map dome (glass)** in Settings > Display (it tints the frame and the glass). PSX and AI PSX keep their concrete walls and don't draw the dome (it still stops you).
  - **The sun**: a stylised low-poly sun where the sunlight comes from - a faceted disc in two yellows round a pale middle, a darker rim, a ring of pointed rays and a soft two-step halo, in flat colours (`SkySun` in `SkySun.cs`, unlit shader `Assets/Game/Resources/World/Sun.shader`). It sits far out along the light's direction and moves with you like the sky (the skybox's own sun disc is turned off while it's up); mountains and clouds pass in front of it. Its colour is in the colour settings. Hidden in PSX, AI PSX and in space.
  - **How the colour settings reach the models** (`WorldColors.cs`): every colour is a named slot (default, saved colour, swatches). Most models are built from plain colour materials (`Art.Mat`, cached by colour); each model's builder wraps itself in `using (ColorSlots.Use(ColorSlots.Workbench))`, and every `Art.Mat` inside it gets a material of that slot's own, coloured `colour x (picked / default)`. Changing a slot only re-colours those few shared materials (nothing is rebuilt, nothing runs per frame), so all of a model's parts shift together and its main colour lands exactly on the one picked. The grass, wheat and flowers (shader globals), pine needles, clouds, sky, sun, ground and the hands read their slot directly. The tint only applies in Normal graphics (PSX and AI PSX still see the original colours).
  - The bases' **team flags** are cloth waving in the wind, all in the team colour (no stripe; PSX / AI PSX keep the plain ones).
  - **The ground** on Highlands (and the theme maps) is **smooth-shaded**: the corners of the 2 m grid are shared and their normals come from the slope there, so the light rolls smoothly over the hills (`MapBuilder.SmoothGround`). The distant mountains stay faceted rocks. **The rocky rise at the edge of the map** on Highlands (where the glass wall ends) is **smooth-shaded again**, like the hills (it was flat-shaded for a while - every triangle with its own face normal - which looked wrong there; `MapBuilder.EdgeRise` still says where it is), and out past the dome the rise is broken into crags and ledges (`MapBuilder.Crag`, the same for every team; none inside the dome, where you walk and the dome's trim follows the ground).
    - **Grass covers all the green ground on the hillsides**: it used to thin out from well before the rock (the rock field from -0.18 down to -0.05), which on the gentler slopes left wide bands of green ground with no grass on them; now it grows right up to the rock and only thins over the last metre or so (`GrassField.RockEdge` / `RockFull`, -0.055 to -0.012). The blades are also planted on the ground **exactly as it's drawn**: the grass shader reads the terrain's own 2 m grid of corner heights (a small point-sampled texture, `_GrassGround`) and goes straight across the triangle each blade is in, like the mesh (`MapBuilder.GroundHeight` on the CPU) - the mesh runs straight between its corners, so over the little bumps the blades used to stand up to ~15 cm under the ground (planted on the smooth height function) or ~10 cm (on the grass field's 1 m heights); and the same little texture carries the rock field at each corner, so every blade checks the rock line right where it stands (`GrassField.RockKeep`) and the grass stops exactly where the rock's drawn (`MapBuilder.RockField` is read the same way on the CPU) - with the field's 1 m texels blended round each blade, a narrow band would otherwise have put a few blades on the rock's edge. `-autotest grass` checks both (roots against the ground's collider; green hillside spots with no grass, now and the old way: on the test map 21 of 5919 bare instead of 731) and photographs where it used to be bare (`grass_*_hillside_*`).
    - **The rock on the Highlands hill faces is clean, not patchy** (`MapBuilder.RockFieldOf`): it used to be each 2 m triangle on its own by its steepness, which over the noisy hills came out as odd rock triangles dotted over the grass, grass specks in the rock and a zig-zag edge. Now the steepness at each corner is smoothed over about 6 m (only the shape of the hill counts, not the little bumps), anything steeper than about 34 degrees is rock, rock patches under about 60 m2 go back to grass and grass holes that small in a face are filled in, and the line between them is cut right through the triangles, so each face is one solid piece of rock with a smooth edge. The grass thins out over the last few metres before the rock (`MapBuilder.RockField`, read by `GrassField`), so it fades into the faces instead of stopping at a line, and none grows on the rock.

## Spears
- Hold RMB to ready the spear and click LMB to throw (a longer wind-up throws harder). Let go of RMB and it's a normal spear again. The throw is timed (minimum wind-up, buffered clicks, a release that can't be cancelled, recovery - see Controls; all in CHANGE VALUES > Spear).
- Thrown spears fly in an arc and stay in the world. Look at one and press **E** to pick it up.
- A spear that hits a player (up to 90 damage at full wind-up; animals take the same) sticks in them. Anyone can press **E** on them to pull it out and keep it, including the victim. Spears stuck in you go into your loot bag if you die.

## Building
- You can only build inside your own base (the tinted square with the grid while the wall is up; once the build phase is over, by default the tinted floor and its grid go and the base is plain flat ground - grass colour, no blades - inside its team-colour border, which stays; Settings > Display > Base floor can keep the grid or grow grass blades there instead, and pick its colours; `MapBuilder.SetGlassWall`). Pieces snap to a 3 m grid. There's no delay between placements, and you can place pieces right where you stand (you get lifted on top).
- Pieces: Foundation (15 wood), Wall (15), Doorway with a lockable door (20), Window (15), Floor (12), Stairs (20).
- You don't have to aim at the bottom edge: look along where you want the wall (eye height or above works) and it goes to the farthest spot along your aim where it can actually go (so it lands where you're pointing).
- **High external walls** (500 HP, like Rust's): a free-standing palisade of sharpened logs 4 m wide and about 5.5 m tall. Place them out on the map or inside your own base - never in the enemy base (no end of the wall may poke into it) and never on or right next to the enemy's building pieces (in every mode, Builder too), and not on the bedrock.
- Rust-style support: walls and stairs need a foundation or floor, and floors need a wall below or a neighbouring floor. Destroying support collapses what's on it.
- Floors (ceilings) can be built off the top of a ramp (stairs) in every mode, and the floor ghost looks at every level your aim crosses - so from low on the stairs you already see the ceiling go green where you point.
- Upgrade to stone (30-50 stone) for 3-4x HP. Melee does only 20% damage to stone, so bring a ram.
- **No wall straight back where one was just broken**: when a wall, doorway or window is broken down, nobody (either team) can build a wall-type piece in that same spot for **4 s** (Wall Rebuild Cooldown, CHANGE VALUES > Building) - the server refuses it and says "A wall was just broken here - you can build here again in N s" (`Structure.RebuildWait`, checked in `PlayerNet.PlaceRpc`). Demolishing your own doesn't count.
- **Doors sound like your base**: opening and shutting a door plays a sound for the base's current tier - its own tier, or the team's fortify level if that's higher (`Structure.DoorSoundTier`): **wood** creaks open on dry hinges and shuts with a hollow knock, **stone** grinds open and shuts with a heavy thump, **metal** squeals and shuts with a ringing clang, **refined** clicks its lock back, hisses open and shuts with a deep armoured clunk and the bolt shooting home. Everyone near it hears it (played on every screen when the door's state changes; synthesised in `Sfx.MakeDoorSounds` - drop `doorwoodopen_0.ogg` etc. in Resources/Sfx to replace them).
- **Picking things back up**: hold E on an **empty** chest or workbench of your team's (0.8 s, Pack Up Hold Time; a bar fills under the crosshair) and it goes back in your bag (`PlayerNet.PackUpRpc`); a tap still opens a chest.

- **Wild horses** wander each half of the map. Craft a **saddle** (it's in your team colour), walk up to one and press E to saddle it and get on. It rides like a Minecraft horse: it goes where you look, Shift gallops, Space jumps. Your view sits high up so you can see ahead.
- **The ball on horseback**: you can get on a horse while carrying the ball, and pick the ball up from the saddle (look at it and press E - E only gets you off when you're not looking at the ball or anything else to loot: **dropped items, chests and bags come first**, so you can grab loot without leaving the saddle; from up there it reaches a little further down for items). LMB throws it from the horse; it keeps the horse's speed. (Cars still can't carry the ball.)

## Combat & raid balance
**Explosions** (C4, rockets, the airstrike's bombs, a fake bomb bush - `Fx.Explosion` / `FxBlast` in `Fx.cs`, local on every peer): a bright flash, a **fireball** (a white-hot core and orange lobes, glowing additive balls ~9 m across that swell, rise and burn out), a **column of dark smoke** rolling up and spreading at the top for several seconds, **debris** (chunks of wood, stone and charred bits flying out and landing) and **burning bits trailing smoke**, a **shockwave** ring racing 18 m out along the ground with a pale shell of air and a ring of dust behind it, a **scorch mark** left on the ground (fades in 30 s), and the **camera shakes by how close you are** (hard right on top of it, a nudge out to ~70 m; further off a small rumble when the sound gets to you). **Heard right across the map, like in Rust**: close up a sharp crack and heavy boom; further off a deep rolling boom (`Sfx.FarBoom`, with echoes) that arrives late - sound at 343 m/s, so ~0.7 s from 250 m - and more muffled (low-passed from 5 kHz down to ~380 Hz at 380 m+) and quieter with distance but never silent (`Fx.BoomDelay`, `BoomCutoff`, `BoomVolume`). Several at once (the airstrike's carpet of bombs) get the full show for the first two only and one far-off boom, so it stays cheap. `-autotest victory` checks a close one (fireball, smoke column, hard shake: `victory_explosion_fireball`, `victory_explosion_smoke`) and a far one (late, muffled boom, no shake).

- Melee lands on the swing's impact frame. The rock is swung two-handed: a hit bounces your hands back up with a short hit-stop, a miss follows through down.

- **Players bleed on every screen** (`PlayerNet.ServerDamage` / `ServerBleed` / `ServerDie`, `FxKind.Blood` / `BloodKill`): a hit with a weapon splashes blood from where it landed (the attacker drew theirs straight away, everyone else gets it from the server); damage with no hit point of its own (C4, rockets, airstrikes, falls, the bomb bush...) sprays it out of the body instead (at most four times a second; lava doesn't); and **a kill bursts** - a fountain of about 70 drops thrown up and away from the killer, a red mist and a wet thud. Drops only settle on things that stay put: they used to stick to a hurt horse's collider where it was in the world, so when it bolted they were left **hanging in mid-air** - now anything that moves (horses, players, the ball, a swinging door, physics bodies) they fall straight past, onto the ground (`FxParticle.Moves`; the same for leaves).

## Project layout
- `Assets/Scripts/`: all game code (namespace `RockGame`). `Config.cs` holds every tuning number.
- `Assets/Scripts/Editor/ProjectSetup.cs`: **Rock Game > Rebuild Scene, Prefabs and Settings** regenerates the scene, network prefabs and materials.
- All art is procedural low-poly stand-ins built at runtime (`Art.cs`, `MapBuilder.cs`, `Airdrop.cs`, `Vehicle.cs`), except the player model.
- Items on the ground (`NetGame.Items`): a stack that gets added to - the Auto Wood pile growing out of the wood machine - gives a little **bounce** (squash, a hop up stretched tall, wobbling back to rest, and a soft knock), on every screen (each one watches the synced count go up; `NetGame.ItemBounce.cs`).
- `PostFx.cs`: the post processing (settings, the global Volume and the extra looks' full-screen pass). `Hud.Search.cs`: the menu search boxes and the typing mute. `Hud.Picker.cs`: the colour picker and the post processing rows in Settings > Display (and the UI's own looks). `DisplaySettings.cs`: the display defaults table (`DisplayDefaults`), self-saving display settings (`DisplayPref`) and the display settings code (`DisplayCode`: COPY / PASTE SETTINGS). `Hud.Fps.cs`: the FPS counter. `Hud.Beams.cs`: Settings > Display > Beams (the settings themselves are in `BeamFx.cs`). - PSX graphics: `PsxArt.cs`; the tree models and their 128x128 textures are in `Assets/Game/Resources/PsxTrees/` (from `tree_pack_1.1`, 36 trees; drawn with the alpha-cutout `PsxCutout.mat`, import settings in `Editor/PsxImport.cs`). Each tree's trunk thickness is measured so the X sits on it, and its bark and leaf colours colour the chips. The tree X chime notes are in `Assets/Game/Resources/TreeHit/` (from the Samples pack, Sound_1 high notes).
- Sound effects are synthesised procedurally at startup in `Fx.cs`. Everything in the world is a 3D sound with no spread and a steep, realistic falloff, so you can tell where it is and how far: arrows, spears and thrown items whoosh while they fly (with doppler as they pass), bow shots, spear throws and impacts carry about 100 m, other players' footsteps are **loud, heavy thumps heard from where their feet land** (out to **Enemy Step Range** 55 m walking / **Enemy Step Range Sprint** 75 m running, CHANGE VALUES > Player), so you can tell which way someone is and roughly how far - **crouch-walking is silent**; slides and horses' hooves can be heard from where they are too, and explosions and sniper shots carry across the map. Hand-drawn item icons in `Assets/Game/Resources/Icons/<item>.png` (e.g. `hatchet.png`) replace the rendered ones. Item icons are rendered from the 3D models at startup (`ItemIcons.cs`); first-person hands and animations are in `ViewModel.cs`.

Networking: movement is owner-authoritative (NetworkTransform in Owner mode). Resources, crafting, building, damage, the ball and its socket, inventories, chests, airdrops, dropped items and match flow are server-authoritative. Melee, arrow, thrown-spear and C4 hits use client-side hit detection that the server validates; the death wand is resolved on the server.
