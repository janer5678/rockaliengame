# Rock Base Brawl

A 1v1 first-person "gather, build, raid" game for Unity 6 (6000.0.42f1, URP) using **Netcode for GameObjects 2.4**.
Both players start with a rock, gather wood and stone, build a Rust-style base, craft tools and weapons, and fight over a ball that waits under a glass dome in the middle of the map.

## How to test

### Option A: two players inside the editor (Multiplayer Play Mode)
1. Open the project and open `Assets/Scenes/Game.unity`.
2. Open **Window > Multiplayer > Multiplayer Play Mode** and tick **Player 2**. Wait for it to boot.
3. Press Play. In the main editor window click **HOST GAME**. In the Player 2 window click **JOIN GAME** (IP `127.0.0.1`).

### Option B: standalone builds (best for LAN play with a friend)
1. **Rock Game > Build Windows Player**. The build goes to `Builds/Windows/RockBaseBrawl.exe`.
2. Run it twice (or on two PCs). One clicks **HOST GAME**, the other types the host's IP and clicks **JOIN GAME**.
   Port 7777/UDP must be reachable; allow the Windows firewall prompt.
   You can also press Play in the editor and host or join from there against a build.

### Main menu
Laid out top to bottom: **MATCH SETUP** (what the host picks), then **PLAY** (host, or type the host's IP and join; the **port ▼** button next to the IP folds out the port if you need to change it), then **SETTINGS** and **QUIT**. The option you've picked shows pressed in with gold text. Buttons blip when you point at them and click when you press them; sliders tick as they move.

### Match setup (set by the host)
- **Players** (one button that folds open the eight choices): red vs blue in *1v1*, *2v2*, *3v3* or *4v4*; or the X-shaped map with 3 or 4 bases (glass walls in an X, the map laid out four ways round): *FFA 3* / *FFA 4* (free for all, one player per base), *2v2v2* or *2v2v2v2* (teams of two). Sudden death is last team standing.
- **Game mode** - separate ways to play; they don't mix:
  - *Classic*: the original game.
  - *Tutorial* (**start here if you're new**): the classic rules (starter items in the bag, everything else once a workbench is down in your base) with the clock stopped and no airdrops, always on the **small Plains map with normal materials** (the menu's Map / Size / Materials rows are locked to that while Tutorial is picked, and a `-map` / `-big` / `-wood` on the command line is ignored). A small panel on the left gives one simple step at a time - a title, a sentence or two and a goal - and you have to actually **do** each one to move on (there's no skipping). **The game is revealed a bit at a time: nothing works until its step teaches it.** At the start you can only look around with the mouse; WASD unlocks on the walk step, Shift and Space on run-and-jump, Ctrl on crouch, C on slide, LMB (and the hotbar strip) on the first chop, TAB on the bag step, the crafting list on the first craft (and it only lists what's been taught so far: hatchet, then building plan, spear, workbench, the workbench tiers - the bow, arrows, chest and ram once you've finished), the number keys / mouse wheel on the axe step, placing pieces on the floor step, RMB (the building wheel) on the wall step, E on the door step, X on the break-your-wall step, the spear throw, the health bar on the food step, and putting things down and the workbench lists on the workbench steps at the end. Pressing something that's still locked does nothing and the panel says "Not yet!"; each step's panel lists what it just unlocked ("NEW: ..."). **Nothing in it needs or mentions stone** (no stone, pickaxe, mining or ram steps; everything it has you craft costs wood only). The steps (30): look at your machine, walk, run and jump, crouch (Ctrl), slide (C), chop a tree, hit the X, get 100 wood, open your bag, craft a hatchet, chop with it (pick it with its number key), go home, craft the building plan, place a foundation, a wall (the building wheel) and a doorway, **break your own wall** (X, or just hit it), craft a spear, throw it and pick it up, pick a berry and eat it (the step makes you hungry first), then **the glass wall**: walk up to it (the ball waits under the glass dome in the middle, where the walls meet), it drops with the dome, run to the ball, grab it, throw it into your machine (**that captures it, which is what unlocks the workbench** - like in a match) and guard it (enemies can steal it), then **the workbench**: "WORKBENCH UNLOCKED" (the notice waits for this step), craft a Workbench T1, put it down anywhere in your base, open your bag in your base and see the new WORKBENCH T1 list (the step also tells you about the Workbench T2 and scrolling the list). The finish card mentions the ball buff, the other starter items (bow, arrows, chests, the ram), airdrops (the 15 second warning) and the grass / colour settings in Settings > Display. A pulsing marker points at what the step is about (at a tree when you're short of wood), and if a step takes over 40 s a one-line hint shows up. While the inventory is open the goal shows along the bottom.
    - **Multiplayer**: friends can join a tutorial any time (up to the players picked) - the match starts as soon as the host is in, and anyone who joins later is dropped straight into their base. Everyone goes through the steps at their own pace. The glass wall drops **once every player has walked up to it** (the others see "waiting for N more players"), or 90 s after the first one got there so nobody's stuck waiting for someone who wandered off; nobody wins by the others leaving. There's one ball: whoever gets it first captures it for their team; the others take it out of that machine (E) and capture it for their own team (each team's workbench unlocks when it captures the ball). Each player's locks follow their own step: `Tutorial.Allows(TutFeature)` / `AllowsItem` on their machine (checked in `Binds` for every key, `PlayerController` and the HUD), and the server checks crafting (including the workbench tiers), building and putting things down against `PlayerNet.TutStep` (each client writes its step there). Outside the tutorial every gate is always open. Code: `Tutorial.cs` (the steps, what each unlocks, the gates, the panel, and `ServerTick` for the server side), `PlayerNet.TutAtWall` / `TutStep` / `TutorialRpc`. **When a game mechanic changes, update the tutorial's steps and gates to match.** `-autotest modes -host -solo -rules tutorial` plays the whole thing alone with pretend key presses (`Binds.TestHold` / `TestPress`, which go through the same gates) and checks every lock (WASD, Space, Shift, Ctrl, C, LMB, TAB, the number keys, RMB, E, X, crafting refused by the server, no crossbow before a workbench, the workbench locked until the ball's captured and its notice waiting for the workbench step) before its step and that each step really completes, and that there are no stone steps; the same without `-solo` plus a second copy with `-autotest modes -client 127.0.0.1 -rules tutorial` checks two players (the host waits for the client to catch up before the glass wall; after the host has captured the ball and finished, the client steals it out of the host's machine, captures it and does the workbench steps).
  - *Arsenal*: normal prices (except the crossbow, 350 wood instead of 500), plus **POWER ITEMS** (in the crafting list once your workbenches are down, see below).
  - *Auto Wood*: Arsenal, but **wood piles up at your base by itself**: a **wood machine** stands on the bedrock to the right of the alien machine (as you look at it from your spawn), sawing wood out of nothing and pushing it out onto a growing pile well out in front of it - 5 a second. Each log rolls out of a lit port, down the chute (held up off the ground on a strut), **drops off the end** and lands on the pile 1.65 m out in front of the machine (`Cfg.WoodTrayLocal`), so you can see them fall out and the pile is never in the machine. Walk up and press E to take the lot: the pile keeps stacking past 1000 and splits into 1000-stacks as it goes into your inventory. **Wood Gen upgrade** (power item, Auto Wood only), two levels: level 1 for 1000 wood (12 a second), level 2 for 3000 (25 a second) - all in CHANGE VALUES > Auto Wood. The wood machine is **futuristic tech in the alien machine's style**, all low - **nothing stands on top of it** (no column, rings, orb or pylons; the top is a finished metal lid with a silver panel outlined in the level colour and flat louvres): a silver housing on a metal plinth, a console with a screen on one side and a spinning saw blade on the other (sawdust flies off it). It changes in front of you with each upgrade: **level 0** is silver with green trim and a team-colour and a green lens in its lid; **level 1** is bigger and goes **yellow** - yellow light strips up every edge and round the base, yellow and black hazard stripes on the lid and the chute, and two exhaust vents low on the back puffing yellow; **level 2** is bigger again and goes **pink** - it hovers over a pink glow on four short tesla legs, pink seams down its sides, a pink diamond and studs on the lid, a second saw and four vents puffing pink (lit pips on its front show the level). Code in `WoodMachine.cs`; the `-autotest modes -rules autowood` run photographs every level next to the alien machine (`autowood_machine_N`), close up (`autowood_machine_close_N`) and a log caught dropping off the chute onto the pile (`autowood_log_landing_N`).
  - *Primitive*: the classic game, but the only things you can craft are the hatchet, spear, building plan and battering ram.
  - *Building Primitive*: Builder's rules (below), but only those four primitive items and no power items.
  - *DNA*: the classic game, but **trees and rocks give DNA** (a glowing double helix) instead of wood and stone - rocks give 1.5x as much per hit, and there are fewer than half as many of them. **Everything is bought with DNA** at the same ratios: wood prices are DNA 1:1, every stone becomes 1.5 DNA (crafting, building, upgrading, refunds; airdrop wood/stone comes as DNA). Every base has a **gambling machine** on the bedrock to the left of the alien machine: a slot machine with a lever, three reels and a payout hatch. E opens it like a chest with 3 slots that only take DNA (your bet) and a big **GAMBLE** button under them. Press it and the menu closes: the lever drops and the reels spin on the machine (everyone sees it), landing one by one. 50/50 (Gamble Win Chance): three DNA = confetti, a jingle, JACKPOT on your screen and **double your bet** spat out of the hatch; anything else = the screen goes red with X's, a loud buzzer, the cabinet shakes, a voice says "LOSER" and a bouncing **YOU LOST** fills your screen. The numbers are in CHANGE VALUES > DNA mode. (The "LOSER" voice is `Assets/Game/Resources/Gamble/loser.wav`, made with Windows' speech synthesiser.)
  - *Builder*: **no bases, no machines**. Arsenal's items and prices, but most crafts take a few seconds: small timers stack up in the top right: the top one is being made (its bar runs down), and anything else you buy meanwhile waits under it with a full bar, moving up and starting when its turn comes (like Rust - one thing at a time, paid for up front). The building plan and Fortify All Walls are instant; you can **craft, build and put chests anywhere**; pieces **lock on to each other** Fortnite style (walls hang off the walls they touch, floors and stairs hold on to walls around them, ground-level walls reach down into the ground). **The ball** can't be thrown: anyone can pick it up, and **LMB (or E) puts it down** right in front of you - a block in your team's colour grows up under it and it's your team's ball. The ball always has a flag pointing at the sky (white when loose, the colour of whoever has it); set **Builder Flag Always Up** to Off in CHANGE VALUES to go back to a flag that only grows out while the ball is planted. When anyone picks the ball up, the block sinks away again. Whoever's ball it is (planted, not carried) when time runs out wins; otherwise it's sudden death.
  - *FUN MODE*: no building phase - the wall is down and the ball is in from the start - and a 10 minute match. Everybody gets the **same** random item straight away and then every 45 s (any item in the game, the unused ones too). Free items land on the right of your hotbar, like wood does (then the inventory). No airdrops in the Fun modes.
  - *FUN RANDOM MODE*: the same, but only airdrop items (the unused ones too) and everyone gets a **different** one.
  - *RANDOM FUN MODE LIMITED*: Fun Random, but you can only craft the hatchet, spear, building plan and battering ram.
  - In both Fun modes the **Game length** row changes to the match length and the free item interval (both - / +).
- **Game length**: - / + for the time behind the glass wall (building, 30 s steps) and the time with the ball (1 min steps). Saved on this PC; the host's are used.
- While waiting for everyone to join, players brawl with rocks on the sudden death platform in space; when the lobby is full everyone is sent to their base and the match starts.
- **Graphics**: *Normal*, *PSX* or *AI PSX TEST*. **Normal is untouched** by everything below. PSX swaps the looks for the PSX asset pack: only the looks change (the same colliders, sizes and rules), and it switches live both ways. **The host's graphics are used by everyone in the match** (a client's own choice comes back when they leave; Settings > Display can't change it during a match). Normal is our own look, where new features get made first. On PSX trees the weak spot X sits right on the trunk you see (it's placed by a ray against the PSX trunk's own triangles), and aiming at it counts.
  - **Items** (held, in first person, dropped, thrown) - each one is held by the same spot the Normal one is (the grip lands where the hand is): rock, stone, spear, bow, crossbow, arrow (also in flight), hatchet, tree cracker (a double axe), sword, C4, death wand, staff of the giant, horse meat, revolver, waterpipe shotgun, rocket launcher, storage chest and high external wall. Items with no PSX model (pickaxe, building plan, portal gun, chainsaw...) keep their own look.
  - **The world**: dark PSX grass ground (Highlands: grass and cobblestone), concrete bedrock and map walls, the alien machine is the PSX alien creature (the ball socket and its arch stay where they are), the distant mountains are the big PSX terrain rocks (the mountain ranges behind them wear PSX cobblestone, with concrete snow; the big boulders are PSX terrain rocks too), minable stone nodes are the six PSX rocks, berry bushes are the PSX bush, horses are the PS1 horse (its ripped texture came out blank, so it's a plain chestnut coat), chests are the cardboard box, the high external wall is the PSX log palisade, doors are the PSX wooden door.
  - **Building pieces** wear the PSX textures: warm brown planks (wood, close to the Normal wood), bricks (stone), rusty corrugated sheets (sheet metal) and diamond plate (refined).
  - **Trees are twice the size** and their trunk collider wraps the PSX trunk you see, so you hit what you see; the weak spot X is **printed onto the bark**: a decal made from the trunk's own triangles around the spot, so it wraps round the trunk instead of sticking out past it, sized to the trunk.
  - **First person**: the alien's own forearms and clawed hands (cut from the player model by `Tools/psx_convert.py`), tinted in your team colour like your body (or the colour picked in Settings > Display > World colours > First-person hands), in every graphics mode. (The pack's first-person arms are human, so they aren't used.) Each hand sits where the old block fist was and follows all its animations. The model's claws are over twice the palm's length, far too long to close round a handle, so they're shortened to about two thirds (the thumb to 82%). Every frame something moves relative to the hand, the claws close onto what's actually held (`ViewModel.Hands.cs`): the held item's meshes - whichever look is showing, the PSX models' pieces in PSX graphics - are cut through along each digit's own plane (in thin strips across its width), the palm is put onto the item (against a handle with the knuckles just past it, or onto the surface in front of an open hand, under something cradled), then each claw closes with all its joints together, a segment that touches stopping along with the joints before it while the rest carry on curling round, so it wraps the real shape and ends about 2 mm above its surface; a segment that starts inside something bends back out first. In a fist the thumb wraps round the handle alongside the claws (or, where there's no room, closes in towards them); nothing curls into the palm. A fist that can't close cleanly (a gun's body right over its grip) slides along the handle to where it can. Tools, the spear, the bow's grip, the guns' grips, the death wand and the arrow get fists; food, potions, C4, chests, armour, the helmet and the like are cradled on the palm with the claws up their sides; the rock, the ball, the ram's log, the long guns' fore-ends, the pistol's side and the chainsaw's top handle get palms on the surface with the claws round it; the bowstring is hooked just behind the arrow's nock. A grip once worked out is remembered, so picking the same thing up again costs nothing. `-autotest hands -host -solo -shotdir DIR` (add `-hands rock_,bow_` to do only the poses starting with those, `-handsclose` for close-ups of each hand from outside, in front and below) photographs every item and the key moments of each animation (swing raised / slam, bow half / fully drawn, spear wind-up, crossbow aim, eating, throwing, the ball) with the old block arms, the alien arms and both together, plus a side view of each, and measures every pose: each rigid piece of the hand (palm, forearm, each claw and thumb segment) and each piece of the item become temporary convex colliders, and it logs the deepest overlap, the hand against itself and each digit's gap to the surface (`hands_contact.csv`, `grip_<pose>.json` with the solver's outlines); it fails if anything sinks in more than 4 mm. **Dead players** are a skeleton on the ground (the PSX dead model). **Hits** leave PSX blood splats on the ground for 30 s.
  - Item icons always show the Normal look.
  - The models are made from the pack by `Tools/psx_convert.py` (Blender, headless): each one is joined, turned to the game's conventions, centred, its textures shrunk to at most 256 px, and exported to `Assets/Game/Resources/PsxModels/` with `psx_manifest.txt` (each model's textures by material slot). The texture packs are 3x3 atlases, cut into `tex_<pack>_<row><col>.png` tiles. `PsxModels.cs` swaps them in, fitted into the space the Normal look takes up. `-autotest psxmodels -host -solo` photographs every item next to its Normal look, the world, building pieces and first person, then checks Normal comes back exactly.
  - *AI PSX TEST* is a separate test mode (Normal and PSX are untouched by it): using the PSX trees as the reference, the whole wild map is redone in that style - the PSX trees, grass tufts made like their foliage cards, and every other model re-skinned with chunky 32x32 pixel textures generated in the game (grass, dirt, rock, bark, planks, brick, snow, sand, fur...) on a PS1-style shader (`Assets/Game/Resources/AiPsx/AiPsx.shader`: vertices snap to a coarse grid so they wobble, textures warp because they're mapped without perspective correction, lighting per vertex, 15-bit colour with dithering), rendered at half resolution with blocky upscaling, and distance fog. Code in `AiPsxArt.cs`. `-autotest aipsx -aipsx` takes pictures of it and checks Normal and PSX come back exactly as they were.
- **Testing** (on/off buttons): **Alien outlines** (any mode, **on by default**) - enemies get a faint glow in their team colour so they're easier to spot, with **Glow strength** and **Glow thickness** sliders (also in CHANGE VALUES > Test; the host's setting is used). **Solo test** - the match starts without an opponent, so you can try everything alone. **Fast timers** - 10s wall/ball drop and a 90s match instead of 5 min behind the wall and then 15 min.
- **Map**: every map is covered by a **big glass dome** (see The map's glass dome below) and has the **crashed UFO** in the middle, round the ball. The big rocks round the border (the distant mountains, some of which reach in past the edge) are solid on every map; on Plains and Highlands **three layers of mountain ranges** rise behind them, each further out and taller, and **big boulders** stand about the map (scenery, not minable; see Normal graphics below). *Plains* (flat), *Highlands* (the wild map: random, smooth-shaded hilly terrain with minable rocks, new every match; the wooden watch towers that used to stand on it are gone), or one of the five theme maps: *Beach* (palm island in the middle ringed by shallow water - wading is slow - with channels down the sides; craft a **Boat** for 1500 wood, put it on open water and E to drive it round), *Canyon* (red desert, tall mesas split the map into lanes, lots of stone), *Frostlake* (snowy hills round a frozen lake; the ice is slippery), *Volcano* (black ash, a broken ring of lava round the middle that burns you, a volcano smoking on the horizon), *Ruins* (broken walls and pillars for cover, extra stone). Each in **Small**, **Medium**, **Large** (1.5x) or **Huge** (2x) (the bigger maps get more trees, rocks, bushes, and horses). Picked by the host; the client builds the same map from the synced seed.
- **Materials**: *Normal* or *Wood mode* (no stone anywhere, every rock is a tree, everything costs wood only, no pickaxe, no stone upgrades).
- **MODE OPTIONS** (button on the main menu):
  - **Airdrops per match** (0-20): evenly spaced over the time after the glass wall drops - 1 comes half way through (7:30 after the wall drops), 2 at a third and two thirds, and so on. None come while the wall is up.

### Power items (Arsenal, Auto Wood and Builder)
Crafted from the TAB list once your team's workbench is down (see Crafting - the Workbench T1 itself unlocks once your team has captured the ball): the sword, Fortify All Walls and the Wood Gen with a **Workbench T1**; the shotgun, revolver, their ammo, C4 and the helmet with a **Workbench T2**. Fortify and Wood Gen show their next step's price:

| Item | Wood | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Sword | 500 | A slow, heavy swing (its wind-up and swing time are **Sword Swing Time** in CHANGE VALUES): 150 to the head, 95 to the body. |
| Waterpipe Shotgun | 2000 | Comes empty - buy shells. Like Rust's waterpipe: one shell at a time (loads the next by itself, or R). Fires 10 pellets that each hit on their own: within a metre they all land for **200**; further out the spread and falloff take most of it. Pellets in the head do x1.5 (a helmet stops them). |
| Shotgun Shell | 250 each | |
| Revolver | 2500 | Comes empty - buy bullets. Hitscan, holds 3 rounds: 30 to the body, 50 to the head. R reloads (from your bullets), and it reloads by itself when empty. One shot draws **one tracer** on every screen (fixed: the shooter used to get two - their own instant one plus the server's copy, which landed a little off when moving or on a client; the same went for the pistol, the sniper and the shotgun's middle tracer). |
| Revolver Bullet | 200 each | |
| C4 | 2500 | The same C4 as the airdrop one (thrown, wrecks the building pieces around it - but see fortified pieces below; stuck right on a chest or a workbench it breaks that). |
| Headshot Helmet | 800 | The alien helmet: put it on and the next headshot does no damage and breaks it. |
| Fortify All Walls | 1000, then 2000, then 2500 | Goes up a step **every time your team buys it**: every piece your team has placed - **and every piece you build from then on** - is **stone** (2 ram hits), then **sheet metal** (3 ram hits, 2x stone's HP, rusty corrugated sheets with bolts), then **refined** (4 ram hits, 3x stone's HP, dark plate with brass trim). Melee barely scratches sheet metal and refined. Each ram hit knocks a piece down one step. |

**Explosives against fortified pieces**: wood and stone go as usual. **C4** on sheet metal only blows out that layer: the piece it's stuck to and the ones right next to it on the same side (not the walls behind it, and not floors under a wall); on refined it only breaks the one piece it's stuck to. **Rockets** do 45% to sheet metal and 20% to refined (Metal / Refined Rocket Mul). An **airstrike** flattens wood and stone and knocks sheet metal and refined down a step.

While you hold the revolver or the shotgun, the rounds loaded and your spare ammo show big in the top left. (The pistol isn't sold any more; it's still in the Fun mode item pool.)

All these numbers are in CHANGE VALUES under "Arsenal and Builder" (and "Fun modes" for the item interval and Fun match length).

### Removing the theme maps
Everything for the five theme maps is in `Assets/Scripts/ThemeMaps/` (terrain, props, palms, water/ice/lava, the boat). To remove them: delete that folder, then delete every line or block marked `// THEME MAPS` (search the scripts for it: Config, Bootstrap, MapBuilder, NetGame, ResourceNode, Vehicle, PlayerNet, PlayerController, Hud.Menus, AutoTest.Modes (the maps test), and the second line of the `MapKind` enum). The `Boat` item can stay in the `Item` enum (items are saved by number).

## Pause menu (Esc)
- **Resume**, **Settings**, **Controls**, **Dev settings**, **Suicide** (click twice: you die, drop everything as usual and respawn; once every 30 s - also `/kill` in the chat), **Leave game**. Esc goes back a page.
- **Settings** (also on the main menu), in tabs:
  - **Sound**: Master, Sound effects (every game and menu sound) and Voice chat volume.
  - **Controls**: mouse sensitivity, and every key laid out by what it does (movement, actions, building, voice, hotbar) with a note on what each is for. Click a key and press the new key or mouse button to rebind it; every action has a main and a second key. Esc cancels, Backspace clears, **Reset controls** puts the defaults back.
  - **Display**: Fullscreen / Borderless window / Windowed, resolution and refresh rate. The game runs at your screen's highest refresh rate unless you pick another; **Use my screen's best** picks your screen's resolution and highest refresh rate; **Apply** switches.
    - **Post processing** (Normal graphics, just on this PC, saved, applies straight away): a master **On / Off** (on to start with) and, under it, **Bloom** (a soft glow round the brightest things - the sun, the sky by the horizon, white clouds, sparks), **Vignette** (slightly darker corners) and **Colour grading** (a little more colour and contrast; no tone-mapping curve, so the art's hues stay put - a neutral tone map turned the golden wheat lemon yellow), each with its own on/off and a strength slider (50% = the default look) and **Defaults**. **Off is exactly the plain look** (the camera renders no post processing at all). PSX and AI PSX never get it (they keep their own looks). How: one global URP Volume with a profile made at runtime (`PostFx.cs`); the main camera's post processing is switched on only while it's wanted. Every value the URP asset's own default profile sets (bloom, tone mapping, vignette) is overridden, so an effect that's off really is off. Bloom is the cheap kind (quarter-res start, 5 passes, the plain filter). Cost measured on the Iris Xe in the built game (`-autotest ui`, on / off alternated three times each): smaller than the run-to-run noise - at 1280x720 +0.02 / +0.07 / -0.18 ms a frame (about 5.5 ms frames), at 1920x1080 +0.48 / +0.26 / -0.47 ms (about 7-9 ms frames), so at most about half a millisecond at 1080p.
    - **Grass** (Normal graphics): render distance (25-270 m, 60 to start with), density (20-100%) and **far grass thickness** (how slowly it thins out with distance: 0% thins fastest, 100% keeps the most blades far away). Less of any of them is faster.
    - **World colours** (Normal graphics): every colour of the world's models, in groups - sky (sky, clouds, the sun), ground and map (ground, Highlands rock, mountains, the map dome's glass, crash dirt (the ball zone), stone nodes, big rocks), plants (grass, pine needles, tall wheat, daisies, lupins, tree trunks, berry bushes, berries), bases (base floor, bedrock, alien machine, wood machine, workbench), building pieces (wood, stone, sheet metal, refined), animals and things (horses, chests and crates, the crashed UFO) and you (the **first-person hands**: your team colour like your body by default - **Team colour** - or any colour you pick). Each row: the colour now, ready-made swatches, a **hex code** box (type or paste one), **Pick** (or click the row's colour) for the **colour picker**, and **Default** once it's changed (changed ones are in yellow). The picker opens under the row: a saturation / brightness square and a hue strip (drag in either with the mouse), the colour **before** and **now** side by side, its hex code and H / S / B, **Revert** (back to the colour it had when you opened it - the hands go back to their team colour if they had it) and **Close**. The colour shows on everything while you drag and is saved when you let go. ## Rules
- In the middle of each base is an unbreakable silver **bedrock** (2x2 cells). You spawn on it. It counts as a foundation (walls and stairs can stand on it) but nothing can be placed on it.
- On the bedrock stands your **alien machine** (with room to build walls behind it, and room for chests around it). It has a **socket** (the glowing cradle under the arch) where the ball has to sit.
- **Spears and hatchets can be crafted anywhere; everything else only inside your own base** (TAB).
- A **big glass wall** splits the map in half for the first **5 minutes**. The **ball** is there from the start, sitting in the middle of the map under a **glass dome** (the dome covers the round ball zone; the glass walls don't cross the middle - they meet the dome and run up over its top, so there's no way round or over). Nobody can reach the ball (or pick it up) until the wall drops - then the dome shatters with it, the ball is up for grabs and the match timer starts (15 minutes, Match Length). Code: `MapBuilder.BuildGlassWall` / `BuildGlassDome` (`DomeRadius` 11 m), `Ball.ServerPlaceInDome` / `ServerRelease`. Nothing spawns under the dome (no bushes, horses, forts or airdrops). The glass walls reach right up to the map's big glass dome (their top edge follows it), so there's no way round or over them.
- **The crash site**: in the middle, round the ball, a **flying saucer has crashed** (on every map, in every graphics mode): nose-down in the ground off to one side of the ball, tilted and half buried, its glass cockpit cracked and broken open towards the ball, torn gashes in its hull with plates bent out and wires hanging, a dead rim of lights (one still blinking), a scorched crater round the ball and a skid furrow behind the saucer, hull plates, a rim piece, a torn-off landing pod, a cracked green power cell and cockpit glass strewn round. **There's no yellow circle any more**: the whole ball zone is **churned-up dirt from the crash** (about 12 m round, its edge ragged, patches of darker and lighter soil turned over in it, lumps of it thrown out into the grass, which starts at its edge), with burn marks, **rubble** - slabs of ground broken and pushed up round the crater (a few still with grass on top), stones and lumps of rock thickest round the crater and along the furrow and thinning out into the grass, clods of dirt - and more hull plates, rim pieces and torn scraps of hull flung out to about 20 m. The rubble is a handful of merged meshes (a few draws) with no colliders. **It's on fire**: little animated low-poly flames on the hull and the ground, smoke rising and sparks spitting out of the gashes (the fire keeps burning all match). The ball sits in the open in the crater in front of it, **in plain sight from every base**: the saucer lies off the lines from the bases to the ball (two teams: off to the side; three: on the side with no base; four: on a diagonal), it's low (about 3 m), and no flame, debris or rubble bigger than gravel goes between a base and the ball. Only the saucer is solid - one convex lump that reaches down into the ground, so nothing can roll or crawl under it and the ball can't get stuck (you can walk up onto it); the dirt, rubble, debris, scorch marks, flames and smoke have no colliders. A ball reset still drops the ball into the middle, onto the ground clear of the saucer. The wreck stays after the walls drop. Its colours are **Crashed UFO** and **Crash dirt (ball zone)** (the old Ball drop zone colour; the stones are **Big rocks**) in Settings > Display. Code: `CrashSite.cs`.
- Whenever the ball still falls from the sky (it fell off the map and is reset, or the dev setting "ball to the middle" once the wall is down) it falls at **half speed** (`Ball.ServerDropFromSky`).
- Pick the ball up (E) and **throw it with LMB**. When it gets close to a machine's socket it snaps in. While you carry it, it fills the bottom half of your screen. Once it's sat still for 3 seconds (not while it's carried or rolling) a **beam of light** comes up out of the ground under it (it starts 4 m down, `Ball.BeaconBottom`), runs right through the ball and on up into the sky. Enemies can take it back out with E.
- When the timer hits 0, whoever has the ball **in their machine's socket wins**. Lying somewhere in your base doesn't count.
- The last 10 seconds before the end count down huge in the middle of the screen with a pulsing red edge and ticks. If nobody has the ball, the countdown **hits 0 and stays on it for a moment** (`NetGame.ZeroHold`, 1.2 s) before everyone is sent to the arena.
- If the ball is in no socket, both players are teleported to the **sudden death arena in space**, Super Smash Bros *Final Destination* style, in the game's own low-poly look (flat colours, no textures): a flat octagonal platform (15% smaller than before: 35.7 x 45.9 m) with pink edges (no lightning bolt), a blue mechanical underside with orange panel lines narrowing to a pale blue point, floating in space. **The night sky is black** and full of a **galaxy**: 1500 chunky low-poly 3D stars (white and a few pale tints, some twinkling), a galaxy band right round the sky made of thousands of tiny specks (thickest and warmest round its bright middle, with a faint glow along it), two spiral galaxies, a low-poly sun and 18 faceted planets in flat colours (banded gas giants, rocky, icy, ocean, lava and alien worlds; some with rings, some with moons), lit by that sun; the whole sky turns very slowly. **The crowd**: 16 floating stands in two rings all round the platform (eight close and a little below it, eight further out and higher in the gaps; none touch it), each a stepped grandstand with benches and glowing pink edges over blue machinery narrowing to a crystal like the platform's, bobbing gently. They're packed with about 960 aliens - the player model, every one its own skin colour (hues all round the wheel, plus classic grey-green aliens and pastels) and size - each with its own animation and timing: jumping, fist pumping, waving, clapping, swaying (whole sections in time), dancing, spinning, pointing at the fight, head-banging, clapping over their heads, and sitting on the benches (clapping, sitting back, or getting up to cheer). They liven up in sudden death, go wild on ROCK! and on every death, celebrate at the end, and every 25 s a Mexican wave goes round; a crowd noise follows the mood. There are no walls: **walk (or get knocked) off the edge and you fall into space** - that counts as dying, just like being killed. Four floating screens show the countdown and the clock (their text is on both faces; only the one facing you is drawn). Everyone is healed, inventories are emptied and all armour and helmets are taken off, then big **Ready?** ... **Set** ... **ROCK!** words (`NetGame.FightCountdown`, 3.6 s) - nobody can move or attack until ROCK!. Rocks only, first kill (or fall) loses / last team standing wins. The same platform is the waiting lobby, where falling off just puts you back on top. Code in `SpaceArena.cs`. Kept cheap for integrated GPUs: the sky is three merged meshes (stars, galaxy + planets, the additive glow) in the unlit `Assets/Game/Resources/SpaceArena/SpaceSky.shader` (the black shell is `SpaceGlow.shader`); the stands are one mesh; the crowd is one ~500-triangle alien (the player model with its clawed hands simplified) drawn once per stand in view (procedural instancing, `Graphics.RenderMeshPrimitives`) and posed entirely in its vertex shader (`SpaceArena/Crowd.shader`: each fan's position, colour, animation and phase sit in a buffer; the limbs bend at shoulder, elbow, hip, knee and neck from per-vertex weights). The crowd alien is baked from the player model's source (`Tools/Alien2`) into `Assets/Game/Resources/SpaceArena/CrowdAlien.txt` by `python Tools/crowd_alien.py` (a flat colour per face sampled off the texture, which limb moves each corner, the joints). `-autotest arena` (host on its own, windowed, then a client about two minutes later - it takes a lot of pictures first; add `-rules classic` if the menu was last left on another mode such as the tutorial) checks the look (no textures, no bolt, black sky, the stars, galaxy and planets, the size, a dense crowd with 10+ animations and 10+ hues on stands clear of the platform, only the stands in view drawn, every arena spawn on the platform), photographs the platform, the crowd (first person `arena_14_fp_crowd`, close up `arena_15_crowd_close_*`, a Mexican wave `arena_16_crowd_wave`) and the galaxy (`arena_17_galaxy_wide`, `arena_18_galaxy_core`, `arena_19_planets`), logs frame times (with everything, without the crowd, and without the crowd, stands and new sky, taking turns), checks Ready? / Set / ROCK! (holding W does nothing until ROCK!, the crowd goes wild) and the falls.
- There is no rock item: whenever your selected hotbar slot is empty, you're holding your rock.
- When you die **all your items burst out of your body** onto the ground, including armour and a helmet you were wearing. Anyone can pick them up with E. Items on the ground disappear after 5 minutes.
- Respawning: while the glass wall is up you always come back on your bedrock. After that you choose: **respawn in base** or **respawn in the wild** (a truly random spot anywhere out in the wild - any side of the map, never in a base, the middle, water or lava).
- **Standing in your own base heals you** slowly (2 HP a second; Base Regen in CHANGE VALUES). Not in sudden death, and Builder has no bases.
- **Ball buff**: only while the ball is **in your machine's socket** (Builder: planted for your team) everything you gather gives **15%** more (Ball Gather Mul). Lying in your base or being carried doesn't count. It shows bottom left, above the armour.

## Airdrops
Each airdrop gets **one warning, 15 seconds before it lands** (`NetGame.DropWarning`): a banner **AIRDROP IN 15 SECONDS** saying where it's coming down (*"It's dropping in the centre of the map!"* with Where = Middle of the map; every side / somewhere on the map otherwise), plus a line in the chat. There's no airdrop timer on the HUD and no other countdown. Once the wall is down, a giant alien ship comes down from very high up (in over the nearest edge of the map), hovers **over the map's glass dome** (clear of the glass, its open doors too - `AirdropShip.HoverAt`; 45 m up, or higher where the dome is), **opens a round hatch in its belly** (two half-round doors swing down, the hole glows purple), and **beams an airdrop crate** out of it, **down through the glass** (which glows purple in a ring where the beam goes through), to a random spot (never close to a base; follow the purple beam). The crate comes down the beam at **half the old speed** (8 s; the crate lands 13 s after the ship appears). Then the hatch shuts and the ship **flies off over the edge of the map, shrinking smoothly into the distance** until it's gone (no popping out). ## Game settings (CHANGE VALUES)
**CHANGE VALUES** on the main menu lists every stat: timings, airdrops, player HP and speeds, damage and gather rate for every tool, node HP, building costs and HP, crafting costs and more. They're folded into sections: click a heading to open it (each shows how many values it has and how many you changed). **Search** (the box at the top, **x** clears it): type and the list keeps only the values whose name or section has every word you typed (any case, spaces optional - `slide`, `bow speed`, `rarity`), with the sections that have a match open under their heading ("8 of 27") and the matching words in gold; it scrolls back to the top as you type and the mouse wheel scrolls the results. - **Import from the file** reads it back in; **Open the folder** shows where it is.

## Inventory
- Wood, stone, arrows and berries are real items that take up slots. Tools and weapons go to the hotbar first.
- Materials stack onto what you already have; otherwise they go on the hotbar from **slot 7 backwards** (never into the empty slot you're holding your rock in), then the main inventory.
- Everything that goes into your inventory pops up in the bottom right ("+30 Wood"); materials spent on building and crafting show up there in red ("-15 Wood").
- Chests and high external walls can go right up against your walls and the machine: if the exact spot is a little too tight they slide into the nearest free spot (up to 0.7 m).
- A chest you open shows up to the **right** of your inventory.
- You always hold something: the hotbar only scrolls through slots that have an item. The rock stays on your hotbar - it can't be dropped or put in a chest.
- **Drag an item outside the inventory** to throw it on the ground; look at it and press E to pick it up.
- Drag to move or swap, **right-drag** to split a stack in half, **shift-click** to quick-move (between chest/bag and inventory, or hotbar and inventory).
- **Shift-clicking things into a chest sorts it**: stacks of the same thing are topped up and everything is laid out in order - wood and stone, ammo, weapons, tools, things you place, armour, food, then the rest. (Dragging things in by hand leaves them where you put them.)
- **Storage chest**: craft it (TAB), hold it and click to place it anywhere inside your base. Press E on it to open it next to your inventory (14 slots). Enemies who break in can loot it, and breaking it spills its contents into a bag. C4 only breaks one it's stuck right onto (not through a wall); rockets, the ram and melee break it as before.

## Gathering
- Normal graphics: Plains and Highlands are a thick, stylised meadow - tall blades of grass, dark at the root and bright at the tip, that sway in the wind and part round players, horses, chests, the ball and dropped items; patches of **tall golden wheat** (head high - 1.2 to 2.3 m - so you can hide in it standing or crouching; it only parts a little as you push through, and there's none near the bases or the ball zone), clearings of short grass with white daisies, purple (and some pink and white) lupins and clumps of broad leaves - over ground of one flat colour (Highlands' steep rock is one flat grey). No grass on the bases, the ball drop zone, steep rock, or under anything built.
  - **Inside the tall wheat (or any grass) the blades right at your eyes fade out**: blades closer than ~0.3 m aren't drawn and they fade in (a fine dither pattern, so they stay solid and sorted) out to ~1.7 m - measured with the height counted double, so the grass at your feet stays. Standing or crouching in the wheat, your screen isn't covered any more. It's only round your own camera: everyone else still sees you hidden in it. (`_GrassNearFade`, set from `GrassField.NearFadeFrom / NearFadeTo`; `Grass.shader` clips in every pass.)
  - **Trampled grass** (like Rust): wherever players, horses, cars and the ball go, and where dropped items and chests lie, the grass is pressed flat (each blade falls its own way, a little paler) and stands back up by itself: it stays flat for ~4 s after the last step and is fully up again ~15 s later. The tall wheat isn't flattened. How: a "trample map" (one value every 0.5 m over the whole map: when that spot was last stepped on) that the grass shader reads, so nothing has to update while it recovers; ten times a second whatever moved stamps the strip it went over, and only the small squares that changed are copied to the GPU (16x16 blocks, `Graphics.CopyTexture`). Each PC tracks its own (it's only looks).
  - Kept fast (it's tuned on an integrated Intel GPU: about half a millisecond a frame at the default settings): one 8 m patch of blades is drawn on every 8 m square in view near you, in one draw per level of detail (procedural instancing); further out there are fewer, wider blades, single triangles past ~20 m, and past ~85 m the same patch is stretched over 16 m and then (past ~240 m) 32 m squares, so the number of squares stays small even at 270 m; the grass shrinks smoothly into the ground (no popping) and is gone by the render distance picked in Settings > Display (60 m to start with, up to 270 m; the flowers stop at 90 m). The density setting draws only the first share of each patch's blades (they're stored in random order, so it thins evenly) and picks the cheaper patch meshes. Blades are lit per vertex. Where grass grows comes from a 1-texel-a-metre "grass field" texture (height, how much grass, wheat, short grass) that the shader reads, so the same patch follows the hills. Code in `GrassField.cs` and `Assets/Game/Resources/Grass/` (`Grass.shader`). Hidden in PSX and AI PSX.
- Normal graphics, the rest of the world (`WorldDressing.cs`, `WorldLook.cs`, shader `Assets/Game/Resources/World/Painted.shader`: vertex-coloured, flat-shaded meshes, lit like the grass, with the wind done in the vertex shader):
  - **Pine trees** are tall (10-16 m) and come in **five kinds**, picked from each tree's seed (so every player sees the same tree): *classic* (four even tiers), *spruce* (slim, six narrow bluish tiers), *fir* (full and bushy, five wide flared tiers starting low), *droopy* (bell-shaped tiers whose branch tips hang down) and *scraggly* (sparser, uneven olive tiers higher up an older trunk with a few bare, broken-off branches). Each has a touch of colour of its own. A tier is a star-shaped skirt of branch tips, a ring part way up and the tip, with a shallow cone underneath. Needles and trunk are one mesh (two draws: needles, bark - tinted by the Pine needles / Tree trunks colours) and **sway together** in the same gusts as the grass (taller trees a little more), shaking together when hit.
    - **The trunk never shows through the needles** (`ResourceNode.PineShape`): inside the crown it's never thicker than the tiers round it can hide (less a margin for the branch tips' flutter), so it tapers up through the crown and stops a little way into the top tier; it's part of the swaying mesh, so the needles can't slide off it in a gust. *Why it used to show:* the trunk was Unity's built-in cylinder, which is 2 units wide (not 1), so it was really 1.2 m thick - twice what the tier layout allowed for - and it stuck out between every pair of tiers (and it stood still while the needles swayed). The trunk is still 1.2 m thick (a flared, ten-sided log) where you hit it, and the collider is as before (0.6 m radius, as tall as the trunk inside the needles); the weak spots are where they were.
    - `-autotest world` checks it for real: every tree's needles and bark are moved on the CPU exactly as the shader moves them (`WorldLook.PaintedWind`) at 16 moments of the wind, and every bit of bark above the lowest underside has to be inside a tier; then one tree of each kind (and the shortest and tallest) is photographed from eye height at 3 and 7 m from six sides, from under it, from 40 m and shaking from a hit, each also zoomed in on the top junction, with the wind stopped at that tree's strongest gust (`trees_*.png`). Add `-trees-only` to run just the trees, the bush, flag, clouds, hills and wheat pictures (`close_*.png`).
  - **The map's glass dome** (every map; `MapDome.cs`): where the castle walls and then the grey boundary walls used to be, a **big glass dome covers the whole map**, in the same glass and frame lines as the glass wall that drops. The map is square, so it's a rounded square: at the ground it hugs the edge (a superellipse just outside where the old walls stopped you, so the area you play in is the same - only the very tips of the corners are cut off), it goes straight up to a shoulder well above the ground anywhere along the edge (Highlands and the theme maps rise there), then curves over to a peak in the middle (rounder as it goes up), 48-68 m high: under the clouds and the sun, over the ball drop (40 m). Frame bands every 4 m up the straight sides and round every other ring above, ribs up to the top. The mountains, clouds and sun show through it. **Nothing gets out**: it's solid (one mesh collider, faces pointing in) - jetpacks, ender pearls, thrown and shot things all stop at the glass - and the old boundary walls are still there as invisible colliders. The glass walls between the bases fill its cross-section. The airdrop ship hovers over it and beams down through it (above). Its colour is **Map dome (glass)** in Settings > Display (it tints the frame and the glass). PSX and AI PSX keep their concrete walls and don't draw the dome (it still stops you).
  - **The sun**: a stylised low-poly sun where the sunlight comes from - a faceted disc in two yellows round a pale middle, a darker rim, a ring of pointed rays and a soft two-step halo, in flat colours (`SkySun` in `SkySun.cs`, unlit shader `Assets/Game/Resources/World/Sun.shader`). It sits far out along the light's direction and moves with you like the sky (the skybox's own sun disc is turned off while it's up); mountains and clouds pass in front of it. Its colour is in the colour settings. Hidden in PSX, AI PSX and in space.
  - **How the colour settings reach the models** (`WorldColors.cs`): every colour is a named slot (default, saved colour, swatches). Most models are built from plain colour materials (`Art.Mat`, cached by colour); each model's builder wraps itself in `using (ColorSlots.Use(ColorSlots.Workbench))`, and every `Art.Mat` inside it gets a material of that slot's own, coloured `colour x (picked / default)`. Changing a slot only re-colours those few shared materials (nothing is rebuilt, nothing runs per frame), so all of a model's parts shift together and its main colour lands exactly on the one picked. The grass, wheat and flowers (shader globals), pine needles, clouds, sky, sun, ground and the hands read their slot directly. The tint only applies in Normal graphics (PSX and AI PSX still see the original colours).
  - The bases' **team flags** are cloth waving in the wind, all in the team colour (no stripe; PSX / AI PSX keep the plain ones).
  - **The ground** on Highlands (and the theme maps) is **smooth-shaded**: the corners of the 2 m grid are shared and their normals come from the slope there, so the light rolls smoothly over the hills (`MapBuilder.SmoothGround`). The distant mountains stay faceted rocks.
    - **The rock on the Highlands hill faces is clean, not patchy** (`MapBuilder.RockFieldOf`): it used to be each 2 m triangle on its own by its steepness, which over the noisy hills came out as odd rock triangles dotted over the grass, grass specks in the rock and a zig-zag edge. Now the steepness at each corner is smoothed over about 6 m (only the shape of the hill counts, not the little bumps), anything steeper than about 34 degrees is rock, rock patches under about 60 m2 go back to grass and grass holes that small in a face are filled in, and the line between them is cut right through the triangles, so each face is one solid piece of rock with a smooth edge. The grass thins out over the last few metres before the rock (`MapBuilder.RockField`, read by `GrassField`), so it fades into the faces instead of stopping at a line, and none grows on the rock.
  - **Mountain ranges** (Plains and Highlands; `MapScenery.cs`): behind the ring of mountain rocks round the map, three layers of ranges go all the way round, each further out (about 70, 155 and 255 m past the ring), taller (peaks up to about 90, 140 and 200 m on a normal map) and paler and bluer than the one in front, so the horizon has depth; the furthest has snow on its peaks. Each range is one merged low-poly mesh (faceted peaks along a wandering crest; the ridge comes from noise sampled round a circle, so it joins up) in the Mountains colour - four draws in all, no colliders, no shadows - and they stay well clear of the space arena's shell.
  - **Big boulders** (Plains and Highlands; `MapScenery.cs`): about a dozen groups per team's part of the map (more on bigger maps) - a big chiselled rock 3-6.5 m across (a ball of faces with flat cuts sliced off it, like broken stone), sunk into the ground and leaning a little, with one to three smaller ones round its foot. They're decoration, not minable (the stone nodes are separate), but solid (convex colliders; trees, bushes, horses and airdrops are put down clear of them). Spots are picked in one team's part and copied round to the others, so everyone gets the same, and kept clear of the bases and their spawns, the crash site in the middle, the straight way from each base to the middle (and, with 3-4 bases, to the bases either side), the glass walls and the edge of the map, and off steep slopes. Their colour is **Big rocks**.
  - **Clouds**: big puffy low-poly clouds (2-3x the size they were, 105-160 m up) drifting slowly over the map (no shadows; hidden in PSX, AI PSX and in space).
  - `-autotest dome -host -solo -fast -rules classic -map <any map> -shotdir DIR` (add `-mode ffa4` for the X map) checks and photographs the map's glass dome (`dome_<map>_*`: from inside at the ground, looking up, from high up, in a corner, from outside; built, drawn, solid; the old walls invisible but solid; over the ground all round the edge; straight up anywhere and out sideways at any height you hit the glass; something flung up and out over the edge, or straight up, stays in; a player flying out over the old wall is stopped; the clouds outside it; the ball drop and the space arena), the crashed UFO (from each side with the glass up and after it drops, close up, from above; every base's line to the ball clear of it; its solid part clear of the ball and low; a ball reset lands in the middle clear of it; a ball dropped on the saucer isn't trapped; from each side you run straight up to the ball and pick it up), the ball's beacon coming up out of the ground through the ball (in the crater and out on the grass), two airdrops (anywhere and in the middle) arriving, hovering clear over the glass and beaming through it with the glowing ring, and PSX / AI PSX (concrete walls, no dome drawn, still solid).
  - `-autotest scenery -host -solo -rules classic -map plains|highlands -shotdir DIR` (`AutoTest.Scenery.cs`) checks and photographs the scenery (`scenery_<map>_*`): the crash's dirt and rubble in the middle (no yellow parts left, the dirt over the ball zone, nothing at the crash site solid but the saucer, no rubble on any line from a base's side to the ball - temporary colliders on the rubble's meshes - and nothing over 25 cm high on the way in; from each side, from above, its edge, the crater rim, after the walls drop), the mountain ranges (each further out and taller, outside the dome, out of the space arena's shell; looking out four ways, from high up, from a base), the boulders (the same in every team's part, none in a base, by a spawn, in the middle, on the way between them or by a wall, all solid, no tree or bush in one; close up and from above), on Highlands the hill faces (no rock specks on the grass or grass specks in the rock - patches joined edge to edge in the ground mesh - and no grass on the rock; the hilliest spots with the grass off and on, and from above), PSX and AI PSX, and times frames with the new scenery shown and hidden.
  - `-autotest world -host -solo -map plains|highlands -shotdir DIR` photographs the trees, the edge of the map (the glass dome; the old walls invisible), the flags, the clouds, the sun, Settings > Display and the colour list, grass at 30 m and at 270 m (also from above, and with the thickest / thinnest far grass), a dozen colour changes (map walls, mountains, bedrock, trunks, wheat, sun, sky, hands...) out in the world and in the settings, a trampled trail right after walking, ~10 s and ~20 s later (recovered), and a player-sized dummy standing and crouching in the tall wheat (and in short grass to compare); checks the walls still stop you and are drawn, the flags, clouds and sun are up, the colours land on the materials and the hands, the clipboard text (logged), the trail flattens and recovers, no wheat on the bases / ball zone, and PSX / AI PSX; and times frames at the default, lowest and 270 m grass settings and without grass. - Felling a whole tree gives a +100 wood bonus ("TIMBER!").
- **Trees show no text when you point at them** (no name or wood count); hitting them still gives the chips, the X, the chime and the hit marker. Rocks and bushes still say what they are.
- **Gravestones**: every time anyone dies (any mode, the sudden death arena too), a little low-poly gravestone goes down on the ground where they fell - **every one is the same stone cross** (the same shape and stone colour for everybody), with a small band in their team's colour where the arms cross, a mound of dirt and a slight random lean. No collider (you walk through them), and they're never removed: they pile up for the whole match and are gone when the next match starts. Falling into the void leaves none. Synced to everyone, late joiners too (`NetGame.Graves`, drawn by `GraveFx` in `NetGame.Graves.cs`).
- The first hit on a tree or rock reveals its weak spot: an orange **X** on trees (a chunky pixel-art X on PSX trees, sitting on the bark you see), a sparkling **star** on rocks. Hitting it gives double resources, and it jumps to a new spot facing you. Hitting a tree's X plays a chime; hits in a row on the same tree climb up the scale (C D E G A C).
- On the Highlands map every rock on the hills is a minable stone node.
- **Berry bushes** (Normal graphics) are a round clump of faceted leafy blobs, each a slightly different green, darker underneath, with clusters of shiny berries sitting on them; they sway in the same wind as the pines and the grass (one mesh, two draws; PSX and AI PSX keep their own bushes). They're loaded with berries: press E to pick the whole bush (it disappears), hold the berries and press RMB to eat (takes 1.5 s, +25 HP). A new bush grows up out of the ground somewhere else in the same half a couple of minutes later. Bushes are fairly rare.
- Arrows that miss stay stuck where they land; press E to pick them back up.

## Spears
- Hold RMB to ready the spear and click LMB to throw (a longer wind-up throws harder). Let go of RMB and it's a normal spear again.
- Thrown spears fly in an arc and stay in the world. Look at one and press **E** to pick it up.
- A spear that hits a player (up to 90 damage at full wind-up; animals take the same) sticks in them. Anyone can press **E** on them to pull it out and keep it, including the victim. Spears stuck in you go into your loot bag if you die.

## Building
- You can only build inside your own base (the tinted square with the grid). Pieces snap to a 3 m grid. There's no delay between placements, and you can place pieces right where you stand (you get lifted on top).
- Pieces: Foundation (15 wood), Wall (15), Doorway with a lockable door (20), Window (15), Floor (12), Stairs (20).
- You don't have to aim at the bottom edge: look along where you want the wall (eye height or above works) and it goes to the farthest spot along your aim where it can actually go (so it lands where you're pointing).
- **High external walls** (500 HP, like Rust's): a free-standing palisade of sharpened logs 4 m wide and about 5.5 m tall. Place them out on the map or inside your own base - never in the enemy base (no end of the wall may poke into it) and never on or right next to the enemy's building pieces (in every mode, Builder too), and not on the bedrock.
- Rust-style support: walls and stairs need a foundation or floor, and floors need a wall below or a neighbouring floor. Destroying support collapses what's on it.
- Floors (ceilings) can be built off the top of a ramp (stairs) in every mode, and the floor ghost looks at every level your aim crosses - so from low on the stairs you already see the ceiling go green where you point.
- Upgrade to stone (30-50 stone) for 3-4x HP. Melee does only 20% damage to stone, so bring a ram.

## Horses and the car
- **Wild horses** wander each half of the map. Craft a **saddle** (it's in your team colour), walk up to one and press E to saddle it and get on. It rides like a Minecraft horse: it goes where you look, Shift gallops, Space jumps. Your view sits high up so you can see ahead.
- **The ball on horseback**: you can get on a horse while carrying the ball, and pick the ball up from the saddle (look at it and press E - E only gets you off when you're not looking at the ball). LMB throws it from the horse; it keeps the horse's speed. (Cars still can't carry the ball.)
- Horses have 60 HP, and a hit on the **head** does double damage (their HP shows when you look at one once it's been hurt), bleed when hit and bolt away from whoever hurt them. They can be killed: they drop **horse meat** (takes 3 s to eat, heals you fully) and their saddle.
- (The wooden car is switched off for now.)

## Combat & raid balance
| | Wood HP | Stone HP |
|---|---|---|
| Wall | 400 | 1500 |
| Doorway | 350 | 1200 |
| Foundation | 500 | 1800 |

- **You can break your own pieces** too - walls, doors, foundations, high external walls, chests: anything an enemy can break, with the same damage (melee, arrows, thrown spears, bullets, the sniper, the ram and explosives). Friendly fire on players is unchanged.
- **Ram**: a hand-held log. Hold LMB for 1.5 s next to a piece (an enemy's or your own): **wooden pieces, chests and high external walls break instantly** (in every mode) - and a high external wall takes the ones stacked right behind it with it (up to 5 in a row; not the ones beside it), **stone, metal and refined pieces are knocked down one step at full HP** (refined to metal to stone to wood). 1 hit per ram. You move 25% slower while holding it.
- Player damage: rock 12, hatchet/pickaxe 14, spear 35 (stab) / **90 (thrown, at a full wind-up)**, arrow 6 (instant) up to 50 at full draw, crossbow 55. **Headshots do x2** for everything (the sword and guns have their own headshot numbers).
- **Crossbow**: the bolt flies at 58 m/s with a bit more drop than an arrow (Crossbow Speed / Crossbow Gravity). It reloads by itself; the reload is **one crank for all your crossbows**: fire one and swap to another loaded crossbow and that one has to wait out the reload too, and swapping away mid-reload doesn't reset it. (Fixed: a client's crossbow used to reload twice before it would fire.)
- Weapons do double damage to buildings compared to before.
- You can't shoot through the glass wall by standing right up against it, and while it's up **nothing hurts anyone on the other side of it** (hits are checked on the server, so hitboxes poking through the glass don't count).
- The bow shoots slower arrows with more drop (55 m/s, normal gravity), like the original bow. It's held like Rust's hunting bow: left arm in from the lower left, and when drawn the arrow lines up with the crosshair.
- Hitboxes (radius 0.45 m) wrap the alien (headshots are anything above its neck), and there's hit assist like most games: a melee swing counts if it passes within 0.35 m of a player, arrows/spears within 0.15 m (both adjustable in CHANGE VALUES, as are the hitbox size, bow speed and arrow drop).
- Melee lands on the swing's impact frame. The rock is swung two-handed: a hit bounces your hands back up with a short hit-stop, a miss follows through down.
- Other players are rigged (a skinned mesh, so the body really bends) and animated procedurally, and you see what they're doing: idle breathing, walk / run / sprint forwards, backwards and strafing, crouch walk, jump tuck and landing dip, **sliding** (low, leaning back, front leg out), holding each item, **drawing a bow** (bow arm out, string hand to the cheek), **winding up a spear** (cocked over the shoulder), **aiming the crossbow / sniper** (shouldered), **eating / drinking**, **charging the ram**, **running the chainsaw**, **throwing** (overhand), swings with one or two hands, carrying the ball, riding, and falling over on death. What each player is doing is synced (`PlayerNet.Action`), so it shows on every screen. `-autotest rig -host -solo` photographs every pose from the front and the side.
- Hits show blood where they land, a hit marker (orange on headshots, red on kills), a damage number, and a bit of screen shake.
